## Abstract

A three-page personal story by Dr Martin Barnes, Past President of the UK's Association for Project Management (APM), recollecting his early project-management career for the fiftieth anniversary of critical path planning (2007) — and, in particular, his first-hand account of inventing the time–cost–quality triangle in 1969.

**Key points**

- Barnes first used **critical path (CPM)** in early 1962 on a dam project in northern England; in 1963, as chief planning engineer on the motorway west out of London (including the Heathrow link), he claims the **first application of CPM to a UK civil engineering project** — huge arrow networks, punched cards, monthly runs on Lloyds' IBM computer using the Mauchly Associates program.
- His **1968 Manchester University PhD** research integrated cost, time and resources in one Fortran program (using precedence rather than arrow diagrams — controversial at the time), showing the effect of work decisions on cost and time simultaneously; a Cobol version, **Project Cost Model (PCM)** with John Gillespie, was marketed from 1971.
- On his **1969 course "Time and Money in Contract Control"**, he pointed out that managing a "contract" (he would have said "project" had the word been in general use) meant managing not just cost and time but delivery of what was specified — and sketched **a triangle with time, cost and quality at the corners**, moving a coin around it on the overhead projector to show the three competing tensions.
- Barnes claims this was, as far as he knows, **the first time anybody had set down that managing a project meant controlling cost, time AND outcome**, and calls it a very significant step in establishing modern project management; the diagram "came to be used all over the world".

**Takeaways**

- This is the origin story, from the inventor himself, of what the book (and the field) now calls the **"iron triangle"** — though Barnes never uses that name here; it was attached to the concept later by others. He calls it simply his triangle, or the Time/Cost/Quality triangle.
- The triangle predates modern software project management: it was born in 1960s UK civil engineering, as a corrective to a field then fixated on network analysis (time control) alone.

## The story (1962–1972)

- **1962 — first contact with CPM**: on a dam construction project in the north of England.
- **1963 — first UK civil-engineering CPM application (his unrefuted claim)**: chief planning engineer on the motorway (freeway) west out of London, including the Heathrow airport link. Produced huge arrow networks written up on data sheets for punched cards; drove into London monthly to run updates on the Lloyds IBM computer (few businesses owned one) with the Mauchly Associates program, returning with "a thick wodge of printer output". "Everybody thought it was terribly clever."
- **1968 — PhD at Manchester University** (after a spell in Canada), researching better money-and-time control of civil engineering projects. Designed a Fortran mainframe program integrating **cost, time and resources**, showing decisions' effects on cost and time simultaneously; it used **precedence diagrams, not arrow diagrams** — "a big controversy at the time". With John Gillespie he later produced a Cobol version, **Project Cost Model (PCM)**, marketed to industry from 1971.
- **1969 — the triangle**: designed the course **"Time and Money in Contract Control"** on integrated cost-and-time control. On one of these courses he pointed out that managing a contract also meant managing **delivery of what was specified**, and sketched a triangle with **time, cost and quality at the corners**, moving a coin around it on the overhead projector to show how the three tensions competed. The concept "really caught on"; as far as he knows it was the first statement that managing a project was not just time control but **control of cost and outcome as well**.
- **1972 — Stockholm**: gave a paper at the Internet (now **IPMA**) congress on integrating cost and time control, featuring his software. Everyone there talked only about network analysis — the first technique of modern project management, whose dominance "held back broadening the skill for a few years". Soon after, he helped set up Internet (UK), which developed into the **Association for Project Management (APM)**.

## About the author

Dr Martin Barnes: founding member (no. 10) of APM in 1972, its Chair in the 1980s, Fellow (1995) and President until October 2012; PhD awarded 1971 (Manchester) for research into improved financial control of engineering projects. Credited as inventor of the classic **Time/Cost/Quality triangle**. Led the team that produced the **New Engineering Contract (NEC)**, adopted by the UK government for publicly funded construction; Fellow of the Royal Academy of Engineering; recipient of the ICE Watson Medal.

## Relevance to the book

The book cites this document as the origin of the "iron triangle". Note that Barnes does not use that name anywhere in this account — the "iron" label was applied later by others; here it is simply the time–cost–quality triangle he sketched in 1969.
