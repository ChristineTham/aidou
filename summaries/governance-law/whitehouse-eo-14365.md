# Summary — Executive Order 14365, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence"

> [!NOTE]
> **Source status:** US government primary document (executive order), signed 11 December 2025.
> This is an executive action, **not** enacted federal legislation. A US federal AI *statute* still
> does not exist as of mid-2026. Treat claims that "Trump signed AI legislation" as imprecise: he
> signed an executive order and later proposed a legislative framework to Congress.

## Abstract

EO 14365 asserts federal primacy over AI policy and moves to preempt state AI laws rather than add a
federal layer on top of them. It stands up a Justice Department litigation task force to sue states
over their AI laws, tasks Commerce with naming the most "onerous" ones, dangles federal broadband
funding as leverage, and directs officials to draft a single national standard for Congress to enact.

## Key points

- **Aim:** replace a growing patchwork of state AI laws with one nationally uniform federal rulebook.
- **Mechanisms:** DOJ AI Litigation Task Force; Commerce identification of burdensome state laws;
  FTC "deceptive practices" angle against laws that force changes to truthful AI outputs; broadband
  (BEAD) funding conditions; a directive to draft preemptive federal legislation.
- **Carve-outs:** child safety, data-centre infrastructure, state procurement/use of AI.
- **Status mid-2026:** legality contested (NPR and law-firm analyses flag interstate-commerce and
  preemption disputes); states kept passing AI laws; no federal statute enacted; a March 2026
  "National Policy Framework" asked Congress to legislate.

## Relation to the book

Updates §5.2.3's US passage (still "no federal statute," but now with an executive preemption push)
and feeds §5.2.4 (fragmentation of state rules as the argument for a single federal standard).
