# Summary — Anthropic, "Statement on the US government directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5"

> [!NOTE]
> **Source status:** company primary statement (Anthropic), 12 June 2026. Anthropic is an interested
> party — it is describing and disputing a government action taken against its own products — so read
> its characterisation of the "narrow" jailbreak with that in mind. The core facts (directive issued,
> models disabled worldwide) are corroborated by contemporaneous reporting (CNBC, Fortune, Time, TechCrunch).

## Abstract

On 12 June 2026 the US Department of Commerce issued an export-control directive suspending all access
to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 by any foreign national, on national-security grounds. To comply, Anthropic
disabled both models for every customer worldwide. Anthropic disputed the stated reason — a reported
method of "jailbreaking" Fable 5 — as too narrow to justify pulling a widely deployed commercial model.

## Key points

- **Models:** Mythos 5 = Anthropic's most capable model (previewed early April 2026, restricted for its
  vulnerability-finding ability); Fable 5 = guardrailed general-release version, out ~9 June 2026.
- **Action:** export-control directive barring foreign-national access → Anthropic switched both off
  for all customers; other models unaffected.
- **Reason & dispute:** claimed Fable 5 jailbreak (reading a codebase to find flaws); Anthropic argued
  the capability is common and that the standard would "halt all new model deployments" industry-wide.
- **Reported trigger:** concerns from Amazon CEO Andy Jassy to the White House (WSJ).
