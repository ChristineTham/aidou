# ABA Formal Opinion 512 — Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools

> [!NOTE]

> Substance compiled from the ABA news release and secondary legal summaries; the opinion PDF is the primary instrument.

## Abstract

Formal Opinion 512 is the ABA's first formal ethics opinion on lawyers' use of generative AI. It holds that lawyers using generative AI must "fully consider their applicable ethical obligations" under the Model Rules of Professional Conduct — competence, confidentiality, communication, candour toward tribunals, supervision, and reasonable fees — rather than treat the tool as outside professional duty.

## Key duties

- **Competence (Rule 1.1):** lawyers must have "a reasonable and current understanding" of a tool's capabilities and limitations; the verification required is fact-specific and depends on the tool and task. This is the duty that answers the fabricated-citation problem directly.
- **Confidentiality (Rule 1.6):** before entering client information into a tool, assess the risk it will be "disclosed to or accessed by" others (including via a self-learning tool's outputs); informed client consent is required for self-learning tools, going beyond boilerplate engagement-letter language.
- **Fees (Rule 1.5):** bill only actual time; do not bill tool overhead or time spent learning the tool.
- **Supervision (Rules 5.1/5.3):** managerial lawyers must set clear firm AI policies and training.

This grounds §5.5: professional bodies now impose an AI-use duty that lands on the individual practitioner, not just the firm.
