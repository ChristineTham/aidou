## Abstract

The *Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025* is IBM's annual study, conducted with the Ponemon Institute, of the financial impact of data breaches across surveyed organisations. This year's framing is "the AI oversight gap": IBM claims AI adoption is outpacing security and governance because of "do-it-now adoption", and that ungoverned AI systems are both more likely to be breached and more costly when they are. The single most citable headline from the extraction: **97% of organisations that reported an AI-related security incident lacked proper AI access controls.** The global average cost of a breach fell 9% year-on-year to **USD 4.4 million**, which IBM attributes to faster identification and containment.

## Verifiable headline figures (from the extraction)

- **USD 4.4M** — global average cost of a data breach, a 9% decrease over the prior year, "driven by faster identification and containment".
- **97%** — share of organisations that reported an AI-related security incident and lacked proper AI access controls.
- **63%** — share of organisations that "lacked AI governance policies to manage AI or prevent the proliferation of shadow AI". (Note: the extraction's wording is "lacked AI governance policies"; the brief's phrasing — "either lack an AI governance policy or are still developing one" — is more precise and likely comes from the full report body, which is not in this extraction.)
- **USD 1.9M** — cost savings from extensive use of AI in security, compared with organisations that did not use these solutions.

## Shadow AI / AI governance framing (as stated on the page)

IBM positions "shadow AI" (unsanctioned or ungoverned AI deployments) as the central risk theme. The page states that "ungoverned AI systems are more likely to be breached and more costly when they are", and recommends organisations "gain visibility into all AI deployments (including shadow AI)" via integrated security and governance tooling. Beyond the 63% and 97% figures above, the extraction gives no further quantified shadow-AI findings.

## Figures in the brief that could NOT be confirmed from this extraction

The following were named in the summariser brief as published report findings but do **not** appear anywhere in this landing-page extraction. They should be treated as coming from the full report body and verified against it before citing:

- **34%** — of organisations with AI governance policies that perform regular audits for unsanctioned AI. **Not in extraction.**
- **~USD 670,000** — additional average breach cost attributed to high shadow-AI usage. **Not in extraction.**

The 63% and 97% figures ARE confirmed by the extraction (with the wording caveat noted above); the 34% and USD 670,000 figures are NOT.

## Recommendations (IBM's, product-linked)

The page organises "take action" advice around five themes, each tied to IBM products:
- **Fortify identities** — operational controls for non-human identities (NHIs) and phishing-resistant authentication such as passkeys (IBM Verify).
- **Elevate AI data security** — data discovery, classification, access control, encryption, key management (IBM Guardium).
- **Connect security and governance for AI** — visibility into all AI deployments including shadow AI (watsonx.governance, Guardium).
- **Use AI security tools and automation** — AI-powered detection and response.
- **Prepare for cyber threats** — tested incident-response plans, backups, crisis simulations.
