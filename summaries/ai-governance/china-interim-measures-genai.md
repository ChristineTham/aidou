# Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services

## Abstract

The *Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services* are China's first national administrative regulation specifically governing public-facing generative AI. They were promulgated on 10 July 2023 and took effect on 15 August 2023, issued jointly by the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) with six other bodies. The Measures apply to the use of generative AI to "provide services to the public in the [mainland] PRC for the generation of text, images, audio, video, or other content" (Article 2), and expressly exclude research, development, and internal use that is *not* offered to the public. Core obligations fall on "providers" and include: content that must "Uphold the Core Socialist Values" and avoid a prescribed list of prohibited content (Article 4(1)); training-data legality — lawful data sources, respect for intellectual property, and consent for personal information (Article 7); labelling of generated images and video under the deep-synthesis rules (Article 12); and, for services "with public opinion properties or the capacity for social mobilization," a security assessment and algorithm filing (Article 17). The single most citable framing: the state adopts "the principle of placing equal emphasis on development and security" (Article 3).

## Instrument metadata

- **Title:** Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services (生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法)
- **Promulgation date:** 10 July 2023
- **Effective date:** 15 August 2023 (Article 24: "These measures take effect on August 15, 2023.")
- **Structure:** 5 chapters, 24 articles.
- **Promulgating entities (seven bodies, per the signature block):** Cybersecurity Administration [i.e. Cyberspace Administration of China] · National Development and Reform Commission · Ministry of Education · Ministry of Science and Technology · Ministry of Industry and Information Technology · Ministry of Public Security · State Administration of Radio and Television.
- **Legal basis (Article 1):** drafted on the basis of the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law, the Law on Scientific and Technological Progress, "and other relevant laws and administrative regulations."

## Chapter I — General Provisions (Articles 1–4)

**Purpose (Article 1):** to "promote the healthy development and regulated use of generative AI, preserve national security and the societal public interest, and protect the lawful rights and interests of citizens, legal persons, and other organizations."

**Scope (Article 2):** "These measures apply to the use of generative AI technologies to provide services to the public in the [mainland] PRC for the generation of text, images, audio, video, or other content." Sector-specific rules (news and publication, film and television, artistic creation) take precedence where they exist. Crucially, the Measures "do not apply where industry associations, enterprises, education and research institutions, public cultural bodies, and related professional bodies, etc., research, develop, and use generative AI technology, but have not provided generative AI services to the (mainland) public" — i.e. the trigger is *offering services to the public within mainland China*.

**Governing principle (Article 3):** the state adheres to "the principle of placing equal emphasis on development and security, merging the promotion of innovation with governance in accordance with law," and carries out "tolerant and cautious graded management by category of generative AI services."

**Substantive obligations (Article 4)** — provision and use shall obey five requirements:
1. **"Uphold the Core Socialist Values"** — must not generate content "prohibited by laws and administrative regulations such as that inciting subversion of national sovereignty or the overturn of the socialist system, endangering national security and interests or harming the nation's image, inciting separatism or undermining national unity and social stability, advocating terrorism or extremism, promoting ethnic hatred and ethnic discrimination, violence and obscenity, as well as fake and harmful information."
2. **Non-discrimination** — during algorithm design, training-data selection, model generation/optimisation, and service provision, employ effective measures "to prevent the creation of discrimination such as by race, ethnicity, faith, nationality, region, sex, age, profession, or health."
3. **IP and fair competition** — respect intellectual property and commercial ethics; advantages in algorithms, data, and platforms "must not be used for monopolies or to carry out unfair competition."
4. **Rights of others** — must not endanger physical/psychological well-being or infringe rights in image, reputation, honour, privacy, and personal information.
5. **Transparency and reliability** — "employ effective measures to increase transparency in generative AI services and to increase the accuracy and reliability of generated content."

## Chapter II — Development and Governance of Technology (Articles 5–8)

**Articles 5–6** encourage innovation, independent development of basic technologies ("algorithms, frameworks, chips, and supporting software platforms"), international cooperation, public training-data resource platforms, and shared computing resources.

**Training-data legality (Article 7)** — providers carrying out pre-training and optimisation training shall:
1. "Use data and foundational models that have lawful sources";
2. not infringe others' intellectual property rights;
3. where personal information is involved, obtain the subject's consent or comply with another lawful basis;
4. "Employ effective measures to increase the quality of training data, and increase the truth, accuracy, objectivity, and diversity of training data";
5. comply with the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law, and relevant departmental requirements.

**Data tagging (Article 8)** — providers conducting manual tagging shall formulate "clear, specific, and feasible tagging rules," assess tagging quality with spot checks, and train tagging personnel on legal compliance.

## Chapter III — Service Specifications (Articles 9–15)

- **Article 9:** providers bear responsibility "as the producers of online information content" and, where personal information is involved, "as personal information handlers"; they shall sign service agreements with registered users.
- **Article 10:** disclose the user groups, occasions, and uses of the service; guide lawful use; and "employ effective measures to prevent minor users from overreliance or addiction."
- **Article 11:** confidentiality of user input and usage records — must not collect unnecessary personal information, must not illegally retain identity-linked input/usage records, and must not illegally provide them to others; must handle access/copy/modify/delete requests.
- **Article 12 (labelling):** "Providers shall label generated content such as images and video in accordance with the *Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis Internet Information Services*." (The labelling obligation is anchored to the pre-existing deep-synthesis rules.)
- **Article 13:** provide "safe, stable, and sustained services."
- **Article 14:** on discovering illegal content, promptly stop generation/transmission and remove it, correct via model optimisation training, and report to the departments in charge; on discovering illegal user activity, apply warnings, function limits, or suspension/termination, store records, and report.
- **Article 15:** establish complaint and reporting mechanisms with accessible portals and disclosed handling timelines.

## Chapter IV — Oversight Inspections and Legal Responsibility (Articles 16–21)

- **Article 16:** multiple departments (internet information, development and reform, education, science and technology, industry and informatisation, public security, radio and television, press and publication) manage the field per their duties, formulating "rules or guidelines for corresponding regulation by type and grade."
- **Article 17 (security assessment + algorithm filing):** "Those providing generative AI services with public opinion properties or the capacity for social mobilization shall carry out security assessments in accordance with relevant state provisions and perform formalities for the filing, modification, or canceling of filings on algorithms in accordance with the *Provisions on the Management of Algorithmic Recommendations in Internet Information Services*." (Note: these two obligations are triggered by the "public opinion properties or capacity for social mobilization" test, not imposed on every provider.)
- **Article 18:** users may complain/report non-compliant services.
- **Article 19:** providers shall cooperate with oversight inspections, "explaining the sources, models, types, tagging rules, algorithm mechanisms, etc. for training data as required"; officials must keep state secrets, commercial secrets, and personal information confidential.
- **Article 20 (extraterritorial):** where services provided from outside mainland China do not meet the requirements, "the state cybersecurity and informatization department shall notify the relevant organs to employ technical measures and other necessary measures to address it."
- **Article 21 (penalties):** violations are penalised under the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law, Law on Scientific and Technological Progress, and other laws; where those are silent, departments give warnings, circulate criticism, or order time-bound corrections, and may order suspension of services if corrections are refused or circumstances are serious. Public-security violations draw administrative sanctions; crimes draw criminal responsibility.

## Chapter V — Supplementary Provisions (Articles 22–24)

**Definitions (Article 22):**
1. **"Generative AI technology"** — "models and relevant technologies that have the ability to generate content such as texts, images, audio, or video."
2. **"Generative AI service providers"** — "organizations and individuals that use generative AI technology to provide generative AI services (including providing generative AI services through programmable interfaces and other means)." (API provision is expressly in scope.)
3. **"Generative AI service users"** — "organizations and individuals that use generative AI services to generate content."

- **Article 23:** where administrative permits are required, providers shall obtain them; foreign investment shall comply with foreign-investment laws.
- **Article 24:** "These measures take effect on August 15, 2023."

## Notes for citation

- This is the *final* version; a *Draft for Solicitation of Comments* preceded it (the draft was notably stricter in several respects, e.g. on liability and correction timelines — the translation page links a comparison chart but the substance of the draft is not reproduced in this extraction).
- The regulation is often described as the world's first binding national rules dedicated to public-facing generative AI. The extraction itself does not make that comparative claim; treat it as external framing.
- The name in the signature block reads "Cybersecurity Administration" — this is the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC), the lead body.
