# California SB 53 — Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA)

> [!NOTE]
> Source: [california-sb53.md](../../sources/ai-governance/california-sb53.md) — Office of the Governor of California (Gavin Newsom), *Governor Newsom signs SB 53, advancing California's world-leading artificial intelligence industry*, press release, 29 September 2025. https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/09/29/governor-newsom-signs-sb-53-advancing-californias-world-leading-artificial-intelligence-industry/
> This is an official but **promotional** government press release, not the statutory text. It describes the law in the Governor's office's own framing ("commonsense guardrails", "world leader") and should be treated as the announcement of the primary instrument — SB 53 / TFAIA (authored by Senator Scott Wiener, D-San Francisco) — rather than as the law itself. Concrete section numbers, thresholds and definitions are not in this release and would need the enacted statute.

## Abstract

On 29 September 2025 Governor Gavin Newsom signed **Senate Bill 53, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA)**, authored by Senator Scott Wiener, described by the Governor's office as "the first-in-the-nation frontier AI safety legislation." The law imposes new requirements on frontier AI developers across five stated areas: transparency (large frontier developers must publicly publish a framework on their website describing how they have incorporated national standards, international standards and industry-consensus best practices), innovation (a CalCompute consortium to plan a public computing cluster), safety (a mechanism for companies and the public to report potential critical safety incidents to California's Office of Emergency Services), accountability (whistleblower protections plus a civil penalty for noncompliance enforceable by the Attorney General), and responsiveness (annual recommended updates by the California Department of Technology). The release frames the law as responsive to California's "first-in-the-nation report" from the Joint California AI Policy Working Group and as filling a gap left by "the failure of the federal government to enact comprehensive, sensible AI policy."

## What the law does (the five requirements)

The release states that "SB 53 establishes new requirements for frontier AI developers" creating stronger provisions in five areas (quoted verbatim):

- **Transparency:** "Requires large frontier developers to publicly publish a framework on its website describing how the company has incorporated national standards, international standards, and industry-consensus best practices into its frontier AI framework."
- **Innovation:** "Establishes a new consortium within the Government Operations Agency to develop a framework for creating a public computing cluster. The consortium, called CalCompute, will advance the development and deployment of artificial intelligence that is safe, ethical, equitable, and sustainable by fostering research and innovation."
- **Safety:** "Creates a new mechanism for frontier AI companies and the public to report potential critical safety incidents to California's Office of Emergency Services."
- **Accountability:** "Protects whistleblowers who disclose significant health and safety risks posed by frontier models, and creates a civil penalty for noncompliance, enforceable by the Attorney General's office."
- **Responsiveness:** "Directs the California Department of Technology to annually recommend appropriate updates to the law based on multistakeholder input, technological developments, and international standards."

Note the transparency obligation is scoped to "**large frontier developers**"; the release does not state the size/compute threshold that defines this class (that would be in the statute).

## Framing and origins

The release positions the law as building "on recommendations from California's first-in-the-nation report… published earlier this year," produced by a group of "world-leading AI academics and experts — convened at the request of Governor Newsom." It describes the report as "based on an empirical, science-based analysis of the capabilities and attendant risks of frontier models," recommending "evidence-based policymaking, balancing the need for transparency with considerations such as security risks, and determining the appropriate level of regulation." The working group is named as the **Joint California AI Policy Working Group**.

The Governor is quoted: "California is not only here for it – but stands strong as a national leader by enacting the first-in-the-nation frontier AI safety legislation that builds public trust as this emerging technology rapidly evolves."

Senator Wiener is quoted: "we have a responsibility to support that innovation while putting in place commonsense guardrails to understand and reduce risk… establish guardrails for trust, fairness, and accountability."

Former California Supreme Court Justice **Mariano-Florentino (Tino) Cuéllar** ties the law to the report's stance: "The Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA) moves us towards the transparency and 'trust but verify' policy principles outlined in our report." Report contributors also named: **Dr. Fei-Fei Li** (Co-Director, Stanford HAI) and **Jennifer Tour Chayes** (Dean, College of Computing, Data Science, and Society, UC Berkeley).

The release explicitly frames the law as a response to federal inaction: "This legislation is particularly important given the failure of the federal government to enact comprehensive, sensible AI policy. SB 53 fills this gap and presents a model for the nation to follow."

## Supporting economic claims (self-reported, promotional)

To argue California's centrality in AI, the release states (attributed largely to the 2025 Stanford AI Index):

- California is "home to 32 of the 50 top AI companies worldwide."
- In 2024, "15.7% of all U.S. AI job postings were in California — #1 by state," ahead of Texas (8.8%) and New York (5.8%).
- In 2024, "more than half of global VC funding for AI and machine learning startups went to companies in the Bay Area."
- California is home to "three of the four companies that have passed the \$3 trillion valuation mark" — named as Google, Apple and Nvidia.
- California is described as "the world's fourth-largest economy."

These figures are the Governor's office's framing and should be cited as such if used.

## Caveats on this extraction

This is a press release, not the statute. It gives the law's name, author, signing date, and five stated obligation areas, but not section numbers, the compute/revenue threshold defining a "large frontier developer," penalty amounts, definitions of "critical safety incident," or effective dates. The release links to a separate "signing message" and to the underlying report, neither of which is included in this extraction. For any claim requiring the precise legal obligation or threshold, cite the enacted SB 53 text rather than this release.
