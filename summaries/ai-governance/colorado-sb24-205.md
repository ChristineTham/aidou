## Abstract

SB24-205 is the "Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act", widely described as the first comprehensive US state statute regulating high-risk AI systems. It was APPROVED by the Governor on 17 May 2024 and, per the page, EFFECTIVE the same day, but its substantive obligations attach "On and after February 1, 2026". The core mechanism is a **duty of reasonable care**: both a *developer* and a *deployer* of a "high-risk artificial intelligence system" must "use reasonable care to protect consumers from any known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination in the high-risk system." Compliance with the act's specified disclosure, documentation, risk-management and impact-assessment provisions creates a **rebuttable presumption** that reasonable care was used. A person who violates the act commits a deceptive trade practice under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act, and the attorney general has exclusive enforcement authority.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> On the effective date: the official page shows the act as "APPROVED by Governor May 17, 2024 EFFECTIVE May 17, 2024", while the two operative duties both begin "On and after February 1, 2026". ## Developer duty (on and after 1 February 2026)

Verbatim: the act "requires a developer of a high-risk artificial intelligence system (high-risk system) to use reasonable care to protect consumers from any known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination in the high-risk system."

There is a **rebuttable presumption** that a developer used reasonable care if it complied with specified provisions, including:

- Making available to a deployer a **statement disclosing** specified information about the high-risk system.
- Making available to a deployer the **information and documentation necessary to complete an impact assessment** of the high-risk system.
- Making a **publicly available statement** summarising the types of high-risk systems it has developed or intentionally and substantially modified and currently makes available, and how it manages known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination.
- **Disclosing to the attorney general** and known deployers/other developers any known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination that the system has caused or is reasonably likely to have caused, **within 90 days after** the discovery or receipt of a credible report from the deployer.

## Deployer duty (on and after 1 February 2026)

Verbatim: the act "requires a deployer of a high-risk system to use reasonable care to protect consumers from any known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination in the high-risk system."

There is a **rebuttable presumption** that a deployer used reasonable care if it complied with specified provisions, including:

- Implementing a **risk management policy and program** for the high-risk system.
- Completing an **impact assessment** of the high-risk system.
- **Annually reviewing** the deployment of each high-risk system to ensure it is not causing algorithmic discrimination.
- **Notifying a consumer** of specified items if the high-risk system makes, or will be a substantial factor in making, a **consequential decision** concerning the consumer.
- Providing a consumer an opportunity to **correct incorrect personal data** processed in making a consequential decision.
- Providing a consumer an opportunity to **appeal** — via **human review if technically feasible** — an adverse consequential decision arising from deployment.
- Making a **publicly available statement** summarising the types of high-risk systems deployed, how it manages the risks, and the nature, source and extent of information collected and used.
- **Disclosing to the attorney general** the discovery of algorithmic discrimination that the system has caused, **within 90 days after** discovery.

## Consumer-interaction disclosure (chatbot transparency)

Any person doing business in the state (including a deployer or other developer) that deploys or makes available an AI system "intended to interact with consumers" must "ensure disclosure to each consumer who interacts with the artificial intelligence system that the consumer is interacting with an artificial intelligence system."

## Exemptions, defences and carve-outs

- **Permitted activities not restricted** by the act include: complying with federal/state/municipal law; cooperating with and conducting specified investigations; taking immediate steps to protect an interest essential to the life or physical safety of a consumer; conducting specified research; and effectuating a product recall or repairing technical errors that impair product functionality.
- **Affirmative defence** for a developer, deployer or other person if it (a) is in compliance with a nationally or internationally recognised AI risk-management framework designated by the act or the attorney general, and (b) takes specified measures to discover and correct violations.
- **Sector "full compliance" safe harbours:** insurers, fraternal benefit societies and developers of AI used by insurers are in full compliance if subject to the specified insurance laws and rules of the commissioner of insurance; banks, credit unions and their affiliates/subsidiaries are in full compliance if subject to prudential-regulator examination under qualifying published guidance or regulations meeting the act's criteria.

## Enforcement

- The **attorney general** has **rule-making authority to implement** and **exclusive authority to enforce** the act.
- A violation "engages in a deceptive trade practice pursuant to the 'Colorado Consumer Protection Act'." (No private right of action is indicated in the summary.)
