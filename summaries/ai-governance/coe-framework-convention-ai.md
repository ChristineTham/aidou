## Abstract

The Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (Council of Europe Treaty Series No. 225; also called the "Vilnius Convention") is the **first legally binding international treaty on artificial intelligence**. It was adopted by the Council of Europe on 17 May 2024 and opened for signature in Vilnius, Lithuania, on 5 September 2024. Its object, stated in Article 1, is "to ensure that activities within the lifecycle of artificial intelligence systems are fully consistent with human rights, democracy and the rule of law." The Convention is significant not only because it is binding but because it was negotiated with and signed by major non-Council-of-Europe parties — including the **United States, the United Kingdom, and the European Union** — giving it reach well beyond Europe. It sets out fundamental principles (transparency, accountability, non-discrimination, human-rights protection), mandates risk and impact assessments, and provides safeguards such as the right to challenge AI-driven decisions, while excluding national security and defence activities from its scope.

## What the treaty is

- **Full title:** Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law.
- **Also known as:** Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence; the AI Convention; the Vilnius Convention.
- **Treaty number:** Council of Europe Treaty Series (CETS) No. 225.
- **Status:** The first legally binding international treaty on AI.
- **Adopted:** 17 May 2024 (Council of Europe). *(Adoption date from the primary Council of Europe record; the Wikipedia extraction states only the signature date.)*
- **Opened for signature:** 5 September 2024, in Vilnius, Lithuania.
- **Auspices:** Adopted under the auspices of the Council of Europe (CoE).
- The treaty is designed to **complement** existing international human-rights instruments, including the European Convention on Human Rights and the Convention for the Protection of Individuals with regard to Automatic Processing of Personal Data (Convention 108/108+).

## Object and scope

- **Article 1** declares the treaty's objective: to ensure that activities within the lifecycle of artificial intelligence systems are **fully consistent with human rights, democracy and the rule of law**.
- Aims to address risks such as misinformation, algorithmic discrimination, and threats to public institutions.
- **Applies to** public authorities and private entities acting on their behalf.
- **Excludes** national security and defence activities.
- Activities within the AI system lifecycle must adhere to **seven fundamental principles**, ensuring compliance with human rights, democracy and the rule of law.
- The treaty establishes **remedies, procedural rights and safeguards**, and **risk and impact management** requirements to promote accountability, transparency and responsible AI development. It mandates risk and impact assessments and provides safeguards such as the **right to challenge AI-driven decisions**.

## Structure and content

The Wikipedia extraction describes the treaty as establishing fundamental principles for AI governance — transparency, accountability, non-discrimination, and human-rights protection. Two contradictory structural descriptions appear in the extraction:

- One passage states the Convention has **eight chapters and 26 articles**, addressing AI governance through seven core principles and detailed implementation mechanisms.
- A later passage says the treaty "consists of five chapters" but then enumerates Chapters I through VIII, listing:
  - **Chapter I** — general provisions.
  - **Chapter II** — general obligation to protect human rights, the integrity of democratic processes, and respect for the rule of law.
  - **Chapter III** (Articles 6 to 13) — the main principles and rights.
  - **Chapter IV** (Articles 14 to 15) — legal remedies.
  - **Chapter V** — risk and impact management framework.
  - **Chapter VI** — implementation criteria.
  - **Chapter VII** — co-operation and oversight mechanisms.
  - **Chapter VIII** — concluding clauses.

- Implementation is overseen by a **Conference of the Parties**, ensuring compliance and international cooperation.

## Entry into force

- The treaty will enter into force on the first day of the month following the expiration of a period of **three months** after the date on which **five ratifications** are made by five countries, **including three member states of the Council of Europe**.

## Drafting history

- 2019 — the ad hoc Committee on Artificial Intelligence (**CAHAI**) began assessing the feasibility of the instrument.
- 2020 — the Council of Europe initiated discussions on AI governance.
- 2022 — the Committee on Artificial Intelligence (**CAI**) took over, drafting and negotiating the text.

## Signatories

The extraction reports that **more than 50 countries**, including EU member states, have endorsed the Framework Convention. Named signatories include:

- Andorra, Canada, the **European Union**, Georgia, Iceland, Israel, Japan, Liechtenstein, the Republic of Moldova, Montenegro, Norway, San Marino, Switzerland, Ukraine, the **United Kingdom**, the **United States**, and Uruguay.

The USA, UK and EU signing is notable because these are not all Council of Europe members (the EU is a supranational party; the USA is a non-member observer state), giving the treaty reach beyond the Council of Europe's membership.

## Competing / complementary approaches (per the extraction)

- The **Munich Draft** (Munich Convention on Artificial Intelligence, Data and Human Rights, draft for public consultation, October 2024) — a scholar-led German initiative taking a more precautionary stance, with stricter data protection, developer accountability, and explicit prohibitions on high-risk applications (mass surveillance, autonomous lethal weapons).
- Other efforts cited: the **OECD AI Principles**, the **Global Partnership on AI (GPAI)**, and the **European Union's AI Act**.

## Most citable fact

The Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence (CETS No. 225) is the **first legally binding international treaty on AI**; adopted 17 May 2024 and opened for signature in Vilnius on 5 September 2024, its Article 1 object is to ensure AI-system lifecycle activities are "fully consistent with human rights, democracy and the rule of law," and its early signatories include the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union.
