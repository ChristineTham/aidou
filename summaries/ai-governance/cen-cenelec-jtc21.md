## Abstract

The CEN and CENELEC Joint Technical Committee 21 (JTC 21) is the European committee responsible for developing European standards for Artificial Intelligence, including the harmonised standards that underpin the EU AI Act. It was established on 1 June 2021 and, per the page, unites over 300 experts from more than 20 countries across five working groups. Its pivotal role is to turn a European Commission standardisation request into harmonised standards which, once cited in the Official Journal of the European Union (OJEU), give companies a legal presumption of conformity with the AI Act's requirements on risk management, transparency, human oversight, cybersecurity and quality assurance. JTC 21 both adopts existing international standards (working with ISO/IEC to adapt global standards) and develops "homegrown" European standards where European needs require them. The single most citable fact: JTC 21 was established on 1 June 2021 to develop European AI standards, including the harmonised standards supporting the EU AI Act.

## What JTC 21 is

CEN/CLC/JTC 21 is the CEN and CENELEC Joint Technical Committee "dedicated to developing European standards for Artificial Intelligence (AI), including harmonized standards in support of the EU AI Act." Established **1 June 2021**. The page states it unites **over 300 experts from more than 20 countries**, working through **five specialized working groups**.

## Objectives and activities

Per the page, JTC 21 aims to:

- Address European market and societal needs in AI standardisation.
- Support the implementation of EU legislation, policies and principles.
- Develop harmonised standards for legal conformity under the AI Act.
- Provide guidance to other technical committees on AI-related matters.
- "Adopt international standards and develop homegrown European standards when needed."

## Supporting the EU AI Act

The committee's role in implementing the AI Act runs through harmonised standards. The mechanism described:

- A **standardisation request** is issued by the (European) Commission to develop the standards (via the eNorm Platform).
- Once the resulting standards are **published in the Official Journal of the European Union (OJEU)**, they "provide companies with a legal presumption of conformity" and ensure compliance with the Act's requirements on **risk management, transparency, human oversight, cybersecurity, and quality assurance**.

### Key standards under development

The page names four headline standards supporting the EU AI Act (without reference numbers in this extraction):

- **AI Trustworthiness Framework**
- **AI Risk Management** — addressing operational risks
- **AI Quality Management System** — ensuring robust processes for AI development
- **AI Conformity Assessment** — facilitating compliance verification

These are to be "supplemented by a number of more specific standards which address specific sections of the standardization request," including standards on **datasets, bias, computer vision, cybersecurity, robustness, logging and natural language processing.** (The full work programme is linked from the page but not captured in this extraction.)

## Working groups

The committee operates through **five working groups**:

1. **Strategic Advisory Group (SAG)** — overarching strategies and cross-sectoral challenges.
2. **Operational Aspects** — practical implementation issues.
3. **Engineering Aspects** — technical design and development.
4. **Foundational and Societal Aspects** — ethical and societal impacts of AI.
5. **Cybersecurity for AI Systems** — secure AI deployment.

(Note: the page labels the Strategic Advisory Group as one of the "five working groups," so the count includes the SAG alongside the four topical groups.)

## Global collaboration

JTC 21 "collaborates with the international standardization organizations ISO/IEC to adapt already existing global standards while addressing unique European needs." This is the page's statement of the relationship to international standardisation — i.e. adopt/adapt where international standards suit (the counterpart body being **ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42**, "Artificial Intelligence", though the page names only "ISO/IEC" and does not cite SC 42 by number), and write bespoke European standards where the AI Act requires provisions international standards do not cover.

## Participation and inclusiveness

- JTC 21 encourages participation from a wide range of stakeholders — SMEs, research institutions, industry representatives, regulators and non-governmental organisations — with a stated focus on consensus and transparency.
- **JTC 21/WG 1** has established a **Task Group on Inclusiveness** to raise awareness of JTC 21 activities and bring in new stakeholders; it publishes a newsletter with the help of the **European Trade Union Confederation (ETUC)**.
- Membership is via a **National Standards Body (NSB)** or **National Committee (NC)**; the named CEN and CENELEC Project Manager is Yannis Chourmouziadis.
