# AI Literacy — Questions & Answers (EU AI Act, Article 4)

> [!NOTE]
> Source: [eu-ai-literacy-faq.md](../../sources/ai-governance/eu-ai-literacy-faq.md) — European Commission (AI Office / Shaping Europe's Digital Future), *AI Literacy — Questions & Answers*, European Commission website, last updated 19 November 2025. https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/ (AI Literacy Q&A page)
> This is a European Commission FAQ (a secondary explanatory document) about the PRIMARY instrument: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the "AI Act"), specifically Articles 4, 3(56), 13, 14, 26 and recital 20. Where the primary text is quoted below it is drawn from the Act as reproduced in this FAQ.

## Abstract

This is the European Commission AI Office's official Questions & Answers document explaining Article 4 of the EU AI Act (the AI literacy obligation). Article 4 requires **both providers and deployers of AI systems** to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among their staff and other persons dealing with AI systems on their behalf. The obligation **entered into application on 2 February 2025**. "AI literacy" is defined in Article 3(56) of the Act as "skills, knowledge and understanding that allow providers, deployers and affected persons … to make an informed deployment of AI systems, as well as to gain awareness about the opportunities and risks of AI and possible harm it can cause." The single most citable fact: the literacy duty is a live legal obligation as of 2 February 2025 that reaches not only organisations but the individual professionals — employees, contractors, service providers, clients — who operate or use AI on the organisation's behalf.

## The Article 4 obligation (verbatim)

The FAQ states the obligation twice. The opening summary reads:

> "Article 4 of the AI Act requires providers and deployers of AI systems to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with AI systems on their behalf."

The expanded form:

> "Article 4 of the AI Act requires that providers and deployers of AI systems should take measures to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf. They are obliged to do so by taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training of the staff and other persons as well as the context the AI systems are to be used in and the persons on whom the AI systems are to be used."

Both providers AND deployers are covered — the duty is not limited to those who build AI, but extends to organisations that merely use AI systems developed by others.

## Definition of "AI literacy" — Article 3(56) (verbatim)

Article 4 relies on the definition in Article 3(56) of the AI Act:

> "'AI literacy' means skills, knowledge and understanding that allow providers, deployers and affected persons, taking into account their respective rights and obligations in the context of this Regulation, to make an informed deployment of AI systems, as well as to gain awareness about the opportunities and risks of AI and possible harm it can cause."

## Who is in scope — the obligation reaches individuals, not just organisations

- Article 4 concerns "anyone in the organisation directly dealing with an AI system." It reinforces the Act's transparency (Article 13) and human-oversight (Article 14) provisions and indirectly protects affected persons.
- **"Other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on behalf of providers/deployers" are NOT limited to employees** — "these are not employees, but persons broadly under the organisational remit. It could be, for example, a contractor, a service provider, a client."
- People working for a service provider or contractor "need to have the appropriate AI skills to fulfil the task in question (same as the employees)."
- Recital 20 frames AI literacy as equipping "providers, deployers and affected persons with the necessary notions to make informed decisions regarding AI systems," including — for affected persons — "the knowledge necessary to understand how decisions taken with the assistance of AI will have an impact on them."
- Everyday example: a company whose employees use ChatGPT to write advertisement text or translate must comply — staff "should be informed about the specific risks, for example hallucination."

## Timeline and enforcement

- **Entered into application: 2 February 2025.** "Article 4 of the AI Act entered into application on 2 February 2025, therefore the obligation to take measures to ensure AI literacy of their staff already applies."
- Supervision and enforcement rules apply from **3 August 2026** (elsewhere in the FAQ stated as national market surveillance authorities beginning to supervise/enforce "as of 2 August 2026").
- Enforcement sits with **national market surveillance authorities**, not the AI Office. Penalties are based on national laws Member States were due to adopt by 2 August 2025, and must be proportionate.

## What compliance requires (minimum content per the AI Office)

The AI Office imposes no strict requirements but says, as a minimum, providers and deployers should:
- (a) ensure a general understanding of AI within the organisation (what AI is, how it works, what AI is used, its opportunities and dangers);
- (b) consider their role (provider vs deployer);
- (c) consider the risk of the AI systems involved (what staff must know, which risks and mitigations);
- (d) build concrete AI-literacy actions on that analysis, adapting to staff's technical knowledge, experience, education and training and to the context/persons involved.

It is risk-based: high-risk systems (Chapter III) may warrant additional measures. Article 26 already obliges deployers of high-risk systems to ensure staff are sufficiently trained to handle the system and ensure human oversight — "Relying on the instructions of use is therefore not sufficient." No certificate, AI officer, or governance board is mandated; an internal record of trainings suffices.

## Extraterritorial reach

The AI Act — and Article 4 — applies to public and private actors "inside and outside the EU as long as the AI system is placed on the Union market, used in the Union or its use has an impact on people located in the EU."

## Note on later amendments

The FAQ records that in the **Digital Omnibus** (presented 19 November 2025) the Commission proposed shifting the Article 4 obligation to promote AI literacy onto the Member States and Commission, "rather than enforcing an unspecific obligation on organisations." This is a *proposal*, not enacted law; the obligation on deployers of high-risk AI systems to train staff for human oversight would remain in place regardless.
