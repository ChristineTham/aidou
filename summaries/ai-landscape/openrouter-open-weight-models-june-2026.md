# Summary — The Open-Weight Models that Matter (OpenRouter, June 2026)

> [!NOTE]
> **Source status:** industry usage data from OpenRouter (an LLM router) — reflects its own traffic mix, not the entire market; still a strong signal of open-weight/Chinese-model adoption.

## Abstract

An OpenRouter Insights post by Chris Clark (27 June 2026) naming "the four open-weight models that matter" as of June 2026 and arguing that open-weight models have kept pace with US frontier labs, holding a "consistent 3-6 month gap for over 18 months" that is not widening. The framing is cost-driven: as AI usage scales, organisations move workloads off closed frontier models onto open weights, where prices keep falling for any fixed level of intelligence. Three of the four featured models are Chinese (DeepSeek V4 Flash, GLM 5.2, MiniMax M3); the sole US entrant is NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra. Figures come from OpenRouter (weighted-average pricing/throughput across providers, June 2026) and the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.1 (indexed 25 June 2026).

> Caveat on the brief's ask: this specific post does **not** publish a "share of OpenRouter token consumption that is Chinese-origin" percentage or a total token-volume figure. It is an editorial roundup, not a consumption-share report. Its evidence for Chinese dominance is compositional and benchmark-based (which models lead), not a traffic-share statistic. If the book needs a hard consumption-share number, a different OpenRouter rankings/analytics page would be required.

## Key points

- **Chinese-origin share of the featured set:** 3 of the 4 highlighted open-weight models are Chinese — DeepSeek V4 Flash (DeepSeek), GLM 5.2 (Zhipu/GLM), MiniMax M3 (MiniMax). The one US model is NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra. No numeric consumption-share percentage is given in this post.
- **Token volume:** not reported in this post. Only per-model pricing (per-million-token, weighted-average across OpenRouter providers) and throughput (tok/s) are given — no aggregate consumption volume.
- **Leading models (Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.1, indexed 25 June 2026):** GLM 5.2 is the **#1 open-weight model at 51** (a Chinese model, ~5 points below Claude Fable 5), ahead of Nemotron 3 Ultra (48), then MiniMax M3 (44) and DeepSeek V4 Pro (44) tied, then Kimi K2.6 (43). On coding, DeepSeek V4 Pro tops open weights at **80.6% SWE-bench Verified** (V4 Flash 79.0%), matching GPT-5.5-class agentic performance. GLM 5.2 leads open weights on the GDPval-AA v2 agentic benchmark, effectively level with GPT-5.5 xhigh.
- **Price/cost story:** DeepSeek V4 Flash is the cost breakthrough — first-party $0.14/$0.28 per M tokens (in/out), ~$0.029/M input with caching, billed as a permanent 75% discount and "roughly 150x cheaper than GPT-5.5's output costs" (with the caveat that DeepSeek's first-party API routes data through China and trains on it; no-train Western hosts cost ~2x). GLM 5.2 realised OpenRouter weighted-avg $0.447/$3.31; MiniMax M3 $0.098/$1.21; Nemotron 3 Ultra $0.423/$2.61 plus a popular :free route.
- **Trend over ~18 months:** open-weight intelligence has tracked US frontier labs with a steady 3-6 month lag for 18+ months, and the gap "has not been widening" — frontier labs are not pulling away. Cost for any fixed intelligence level keeps dropping.
- **Geopolitical context:** GLM 5.2 landed days after a US export-control directive that forced Anthropic to broadly disable Fable 5 and Mythos 5 to prevent foreign-national access — making an MIT-licensed near-frontier open model more attractive for organisations demanding continuity. NVIDIA's Nemotron is framed as a deliberate US/vendor counterweight, seeding open models to drive demand for its inference stack.
