# Position: Coding Benchmarks Are Misaligned with Agentic Software Engineering

> Source: arXiv:2606.17799 (2026-06-16). Gorinova, M. I., Baker, M., Heineike, A., Shaposhnikov, M., Willoughby, R., & Knox, D. (Tessl), et al. PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2606.17799-benchmarks-misaligned-agentic-se.pdf`.

## Thesis
Current coding benchmarks (SWE-Bench, HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench, BigCodeBench) were designed in a pre-agent era: they collapse model, harness, and environment into a single end-to-end score, graded against one reference solution, with no component-level signal. But a coding agent in practice is not a model — it is a "system harness," a composite of models, scaffolds, contexts, environments, and feedback loops, any one of which can move the score by margins comparable to those between model generations. The paper argues benchmarks must therefore be redesigned around the structure of agentic systems and grounded in behavioural specifications rather than reference patches.

## Key findings
- Across four agent harnesses running the *same* model (Claude Opus 4.6) on the fixed TerminalBench task distribution, success rates vary by 20+ percentage points (79.8% for ForgeCode down to 58.0% for Claude Code), a range comparable to differences between model generations (Table 1, p.3).
- Real-world PR acceptance is far below headline benchmark numbers: Li et al.'s AIDev dataset of 456k agent-authored PRs across 61k repositories shows acceptance rates of 35–64%, versus the >70% figures reported on SWE-Bench Verified (p.2).
- Differential testing (Wang et al.) finds 7.8% of "resolved" SWE-Bench patches fail developer-written tests and 29.6% diverge from the gold patch's runtime behaviour; SWE-Bench+ (Aleithan et al.) reports 32.67% solution leakage in issue text and 31.08% passes under insufficient tests (pp.2–3).
- Scaffold effectiveness is not an inherent property: Fan et al. (SWE-Effi) report the same scaffold varies 3–7× in token-budget effectiveness across base LLMs, with model swaps moving resolve rate 2–3× at fixed scaffold (pp.2–3).
- Across 200,000+ SWE-Bench runs (AI21), orchestration choices, container allocations, and evaluation seeds materially move the pass rate at fixed model and fixed harness; Anthropic report similar infrastructure-level noise internally (p.3).
- Whitfill et al. find many "resolved" patches would not be merged under ordinary maintainer review — passing hidden tests grades local behaviour, not design quality, abstraction choice, or architectural fit (p.4).

## Relevance to the book
Chapter 3's discussion of AI-native software engineering and "code quality vs. mergeability" gets a peer-reviewed empirical anchor here: the paper quantifies exactly the gap between "passes the tests" and "would actually merge," and echoes Hassan et al.'s "SE 3.0" call that "passing tests alone is no longer enough," replacing test-pass-as-success with merge-readiness. It supplies hard numbers to balance Ahuja's opinion writing and directly supports the intent-driven / spec-driven thread: the authors identify *spec quality*, not model capability, as the primary bottleneck, and frame the central open problem as operationalisation — stating *what* we want without prescribing *how*.

## Stance
quality-skeptic (with AI-native-framing) — argues that end-to-end pass rates systematically overstate real agent quality and mergeability, that single-reference grading cannot see design-level quality, and that agentic SE needs measurement of the whole composite system rather than the model alone. Sympathetic to spec/invariant-driven evaluation, so also partly pro-SDD.
