# LLM-Assisted Repository-Level Generation with Structured Spec-Driven Engineering

> Source: arXiv:2605.02455 (2026-05-04). Shuzhao Feng, Boqi Chen, Brett H. Meyer, and Gunter Mussbacher. PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2605.02455-repo-level-structured-spec-driven.pdf`.

## Thesis
LLMs generate isolated functions well but degrade sharply at repository scale, and natural-language prompts are ambiguous and hard to verify. The authors propose *structured spec-driven engineering* (SSDE), which feeds LLMs structured artifacts (Gherkin specifications, domain models, and model-layer signatures) instead of, or alongside, prose. A pilot study generating MVC business logic argues that structured inputs make high-quality, verifiable, repository-level generation a tangible goal. This is a vision/pilot paper (FSE Companion '26), not a large-scale empirical study.

## Key findings
- Adding any type of structured specification significantly raises the test pass rate (TPR) over a natural-language-only baseline; the natural-language-only, no-model baseline was as low as 0.0% TPR for some Claude configurations (Table 2, p. 4).
- Providing model-layer *signatures* (API) rather than the raw domain model improved average TPR by +7.82% and lowered standard deviation by 2.47% (p. 3).
- Gherkin specs averaged 6.8% lower TPR than natural-language specs overall, but beat them in 14 of 30 combinations, by +7.7% on average where they won — suggesting headroom, not superiority yet (p. 3).
- Claude Sonnet 4.5 was the best model, exceeding 80% configuration-average TPR on 4 of 6 samples and reaching perfect TPR on some individual runs (pp. 3-4).
- High variance is common: outputs show large TPR standard deviations driven by LLM stochasticity, with single error types propagating into batches of test failures (p. 3).
- Over 70% of failures are statically detectable (e.g., invoking non-existent API 49.0%, data-type mismatch 20.2%), so a static-analysis feedback loop could plausibly fix most errors automatically (Table 3, p. 4).

## Relevance to the book
This peer-reviewed source complicates Ahuja's "SDD collapses at scale" claim rather than confirming it. Its starting premise agrees that *unstructured* natural-language prompting fails at repository scale — but its finding is that adding structured specs is precisely what recovers quality (from near-0% baselines to 80%+), so the collapse is attributed to prose ambiguity, not to spec-driven method. It is honest about limits (Gherkin still trails NL on average, high variance, real maintenance cost, single-shot pilot on three systems), giving the book a balanced, empirically grounded counterweight to a single opinion writer.

## Stance
pro-SDD (empirically framed) — argues structured, verifiable specifications are the path to reliable repository-scale generation, while candidly reporting that the naive spec approach is not yet flawless and net productivity gains remain unquantified.
