# 2508.15734 — Measuring the Environmental Impact of Delivering AI at Google Scale

> Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15734 — Elsworth, Huang, Patterson, Schneider, Sedivy, Goodman, Townsend, Ranganathan, Dean, Vahdat, Gomes & Manyika (Google, 2025). PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2508.15734-ai-environmental-impact-google.pdf`.

## Thesis

Google proposes a full-stack measurement methodology for the per-prompt environmental cost of AI inference — counting not just the active AI accelerator, but host CPU/DRAM, idle/reserved machine capacity, and data-centre overhead (PUE, water). Using May 2025 production data, the median Gemini Apps text prompt consumed 0.24 Wh of energy, emitted 0.03 gCO2e, and used 0.26 mL of water (about five drops) — energy equivalent to under nine seconds of TV. Over 12 months the median per-prompt energy and carbon fell by 33x and 44x respectively via software efficiency and cleaner energy. The lesson: comprehensive boundary-drawing matters enormously (their figure is far below narrower public estimates), and per-unit inference cost is dropping fast.

## Relevance to the book

Balances the Chapter 5 environmental subsection by pairing the IEA's rising aggregate demand with the per-inference micro view. It shows the two forces in tension: individual queries are cheap and getting cheaper (33x/44x in a year), yet total demand is climbing (IEA). This sets up the Jevons-paradox / rebound discussion — efficiency per prompt does not guarantee lower total consumption if usage scales faster. Note the source is a Google-authored paper (self-reported, favourable framing, methodological choices that lower the number); the book should cite it as a vendor measurement, cross-checked against the independent IEA figures rather than taken alone.
