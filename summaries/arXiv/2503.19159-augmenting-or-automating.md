# 2503.19159 — Augmenting or Automating Labor? The Effect of AI Development on New Work, Employment, and Wages

> Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19159 — David Marguerit (LISER, March 2025). PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2503.19159-augmenting-or-automating.pdf`.

## Thesis
Using novel occupation- and industry-level indices of "automation AI" vs "augmentation AI" built from Stack Overflow questions (2015–2022, US), with instrumental-variable estimation, the study finds the two do opposite things to labour. A one-standard-deviation rise in augmentation-AI exposure raises employment by 3.1% and spurs new work and higher wages, concentrated in high-skilled occupations; a one-SD rise in automation-AI exposure cuts hourly wages by 7.7% and reduces new work and employment in low-skilled occupations (middle-skilled are hurt by automation but helped by augmentation). The lesson: AI is not one force on jobs — whether it augments or automates a task determines whether wages and work rise or fall, and the split is pushing toward wider wage inequality.

## Relevance to the book
Anchors Ch6's future-of-work / labour-economics section with a task-level augmentation-vs-automation distinction and hard elasticities (+3.1% employment; −7.7% wages per SD). It complements — rather than duplicates — Ch6's existing Brynjolfsson et al. (2023) call-centre result and the Stanford HAI augmentation point: those show augmentation gains within a job, while Marguerit shows the *distributional* fork across occupations and the counterweight that automation-heavy AI depresses wages and new work for lower-skilled workers.
