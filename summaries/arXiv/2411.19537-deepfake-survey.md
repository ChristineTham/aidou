# 2411.19537 — Deepfake Media Generation and Detection in the Generative AI Era: A Survey and Outlook

> Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19537 — Croitoru, Hîji, Hondru, Ristea, Irofti, Popescu, Rusu, Ionescu, Khan & Shah (2024; ACM Computing Surveys, 2026). PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2411.19537-deepfake-survey.pdf`.

## Thesis

This survey maps the generative-AI arms race in synthetic media: it catalogues modern deepfake generation methods (GANs, diffusion models, NeRF) across image, video and audio, alongside the detection methods built to catch them, and constructs a multimodal out-of-distribution benchmark to stress-test detectors. Its central empirical finding is that state-of-the-art detectors fail to generalize to deepfakes produced by generators they were not trained on — the "generalization dilemma" — so a detector that scores well on known fakes degrades sharply on content from a new, unseen model. The lesson: detection is structurally one step behind generation, and any authenticity strategy that relies on post-hoc detection alone is brittle.

## Relevance to the book

Supports the Chapter 5 subsection on misinformation, deepfakes and content authenticity. Chapter 5 already cites C2PA content credentials; this survey supplies the "why provenance, not just detection" argument — because passive detection provably fails to generalize across generators, the burden shifts to proactive provenance/watermarking (C2PA) rather than after-the-fact classifiers. It adds needed balance: it is a peer-reviewed (ACM Computing Surveys) synthesis rather than a vendor claim, and it documents both the growing realism of diffusion-era deepfakes and the concrete limits of the tools meant to police them.
