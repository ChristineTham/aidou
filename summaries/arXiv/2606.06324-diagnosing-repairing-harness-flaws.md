# From Failed Trajectories to Reliable LLM Agents: Diagnosing and Repairing Harness Flaws

> Source: arXiv:2606.06324 (v2, 2 Jul 2026). Chen, Wang, Liu, Wang, Zheng, Wang (Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Tianjin University). PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2606.06324-diagnosing-repairing-harness-flaws.pdf`.

## Thesis
Many LLM-agent failures are bugs in the *agent harness* — the runtime infrastructure wrapping the base model — not failures of the model's reasoning. The paper introduces HARNESSFIX, a trace-grounded, diagnosis-driven framework that compiles raw execution traces plus harness artifacts into a Harness-aware Trace Intermediate Representation (HTIR), attributes each failure to responsible runtime steps and specific harness layers (using the seven-layer ETCLOVG taxonomy), then applies *scoped* repair operators to patch the harness under regression-aware validation. The claim: treat failed trajectories as structured diagnostic evidence, not just outcome feedback, and reliability recovers substantially without touching model weights.

## Key findings
- ETCLOVG = the seven-layer harness taxonomy (from the cited harness-engineering survey [1], Table I): **E**xecution Environment & Sandbox, **T**ool Interface, **C**ontext & Memory, **L**ifecycle & Orchestration, **O**bservability, **V**erification & Evaluation, **G**overnance & Security. (Note the acronym letters map to Execution, Tooling, Context, Lifecycle, Observability, Verification, Governance.)
- Empirical study of **30 popular open-source agent repos** and **~57,780 development records**: harness-related changes appear in **all 30 repos** and account for **~45.3%** of all development records (26,174 harness-related records). Harness flaws span all seven layers — not confined to prompts or one component (pp. 2-3).
- Flaw distribution across ETCLOVG (Fig. 1b): Lifecycle 25.4%, Tooling 21.5%, Observability 18.1%, Context 13.2%, Execution 9.3%, Verification 7.0%, Governance 5.5%. Lifecycle, Tooling, and Observability are the top three, with **29 of 30 agents** exhibiting flaws in these three layers.
- Reliability recovery: over the initial harnesses HARNESSFIX improves task completion rate by **6.3%-18.4%** across four benchmarks and five models, averaging **+11.1%** (Table III). Largest gains on GAIA (+14.5 to +18.4 pts); smallest on AppWorld (+5.9 to +9.3). Per-model average gains 10.3%-12.5% — model-consistent.
- Beats baselines (GPT-5 mini, Table IV): **+6.3% avg over human-designed harnesses** (range 1.9-10.0) with zero manual design effort, and **+6.9% avg over automated self-evolution baselines** (GEPA, SCOPE, ReCreate, Meta-Harness; range 2.6-16.7). Even vs. the strongest baseline Meta-Harness, HARNESSFIX is 2.6-5.0 pts higher while Meta-Harness burns 63.5%-100.5% more repair tokens. p-values 2.4e-4 to 4.9e-4 (all < 0.001).
- Diagnosis quality (RQ2, Table V): full HTIR hits **85.0% step-localization accuracy, 83.8% root-cause, 81.3% implementation-anchor, 86.2% harness-layer macro-F1, 82.5% repair-operator accuracy** — vs. ~45-52% step accuracy for prior failure-attribution techniques. Raw-trace-only baseline drops to 55.0% step accuracy, confirming the data-flow/control-flow links drive the gains.
- Ablations (Table VI): every design choice matters; the biggest degradation comes from prompt-only repair or removing scoped repair operators, showing that fixing runtime mechanisms (not just prompts) is what recovers reliability.
- Cross-model transfer (RQ4, Table VII): a GAIA harness repaired with GPT-5 mini, reused unchanged on four other models, still yields +5.5% to +9.5% — evidence the repaired flaws are harness-level and shared across models, not model-specific.

## Relevance to the book
"AI-dō" is a practical guide to working with AI; this paper directly supports expanding Chapter 4 §4.2.1 "Harness Engineering". It uniquely contributes (1) a concrete, named seven-layer taxonomy of the harness (ETCLOVG) that gives readers a checklist for where things go wrong, and (2) hard evidence — from 30 real repos and 57,780 dev records — that a large share (~45%) of agent development effort and many runtime failures are harness bugs, not model failures. Crucially it shows repairing the harness alone recovers reliability by 6-18 percentage points and transfers across models, reinforcing the book's argument that engineering the scaffolding around the model is often more practical and higher-leverage than swapping or fine-tuning the model.

## Stance
Endorse (with citation) — strong primary evidence that the harness, not the model, is where much agent unreliability lives and can be fixed.
