## Abstract

Using a real-money prediction market (Polymarket) as an externally-resolved benchmark, this pilot shows that the value of human–AI collaboration in forecasting depends on a specific, measurable form of human capital. At the individual level, hybrid performance is **trimodal**: most people either deferred to the model (Automators) or used it to rubber-stamp a prior guess (Validators, who did *worse* than the model or even the unaided human), while a minority (Cyborgs) reasoned complementarily and matched or beat the market. **Collaborative traits — perspective-taking, intellectual humility, curiosity — not raw cognitive ability or model benchmarks — distinguished who reached the Cyborg mode.**

**Key points**

- **Setting:** a **real-money Polymarket prediction market** as objective ground truth — 30 live contracts resolving Nov 2025–Jan 2026 (economics, international relations, business). Every forecast scored on a common, style-free metric (Brier score, scaled ×100; lower is better).
- **Sample:** 108 recruited (Berkeley, CA; $20/session × 3); main study 78 participants in 26 three-person teams (12 human-only, 14 hybrid); 77 analysed after one exclusion. Four models forecast independently as an AI-only baseline: Llama 3.1 (8B), Qwen3 (8B), GPT-4o, Gemini 3 Pro.
- **Three emergent collaboration behaviours/styles:** **Automators** (adopt the model's answer with little added reasoning), **Validators** (use the model to check a prior guess), **Cyborgs** (iterative complementary reasoning).
- **Trimodal "K-shape" of accuracy (scaled Brier, lower is better):** Human-only 14.7; Automators 10.4; **Validators 31.7** (significantly *worse* than humans alone, p = .017); **Cyborgs 3.8** (beat every individual model; matched/beat the market benchmark of 3.5). AI-only mean baseline 5.5.
- **Human capital, not benchmarks, predicts hybrid success:** cognitive ability did **not** predict hybrid accuracy (g: r = −0.10; fluid reasoning: r = −0.08; both n.s.). Collaborative traits did: **perspective-taking r = −0.32, p = .04**, with curiosity (r = −0.26) and intellectual humility (r = −0.25) trending.
- **Who becomes a Cyborg** is sharply gated by those same traits: Cyborgs vs. other hybrids on intellectual humility (6.5 vs. 4.4, d = 4.1), curiosity (6.7 vs. 5.1, d = 4.0), perspective-taking (6.5 vs. 4.6, d = 3.2).

**Takeaways**

- Reporting human–AI collaboration as a single average hides a strongly trimodal outcome — including a mode (Validator) that underperforms both the AI and the unaided human.
- **"What makes a model score well, or a person reason well alone, is not what makes a human–AI team effective."** AI access decouples raw ability from outcome (the model supplies a floor); collaborative capacity governs who transcends it.

## Introduction

Studies of human–AI collaboration report conflicting effects (augmentation of the low-skilled; benefits accruing to the experienced; capable models degrading human performance; large returns on elite tasks). A recurring confound is **evaluation**: AI may have encoded the problem (contamination), and "quality" is often scored by expert raters swayed by fluent AI-assisted prose. Forecasting against a real-money prediction market sidesteps both — every prediction is scored against externally-resolved ground truth on a common metric. The pilot asks (i) whether human capital moderates the value of AI assistance, and (ii) whether distinct interaction styles mediate that moderation. All analyses are at the individual-forecaster level.

## Methods

- **Participants/design:** 108 recruited by flyer (Berkeley), $20/session × 3. Main study: 78 participants (42 UC Berkeley students, 36 community adults; mean age 28.7, range 18–60) in 26 three-person teams — 12 human-only, 14 hybrid (each hybrid team using one of four LLMs). A separate 30-participant pool used a "Socratic" model (reported in S1). Teams collaborated but each entered an individual forecast. One participant excluded for an out-of-range Brier score; 77 analysed.
- **Task/ground truth:** 30 live Polymarket contracts resolving Nov 2025–Jan 2026; each team forecast 10 drawn at random. Externally certified resolution (o ∈ {0,1}) is ground truth. The four models forecast all 30 (AI-only baseline). Market implied probability recorded as external reference (scaled Brier 3.5).
- **Outcome metric:** Brier score B = (1/N) Σ (pᵢ − oᵢ)², scaled ×100 (0–100, lower is better; an uninformative 0.5 forecast scores 25).
- **Human-capital measures (Session 1):** Raven's Advanced Progressive Matrices Set II (fluid reasoning), the ICAR g proxy, IRI Perspective-Taking subscale, Comprehensive Intellectual Humility Scale, an epistemic-curiosity scale, Brief Resilience Scale, TIPI Big-Five.
- **Interaction styles:** classified *post hoc* from process into Automators / Validators / Cyborgs. Because style is emergent (not assigned), it is collinear with human capital, so style comparisons are descriptive.
- **Analysis:** participant-level means with 95% CIs; Kruskal–Wallis omnibus; Welch's t-tests for pairwise; Pearson correlations of human capital with accuracy; Cohen's d.

## Results

### A trimodal "K-shape"

Forecasting error differed sharply across conditions (Kruskal–Wallis χ², p < 10⁻¹³).

| Group | Scaled Brier (lower = better) | 95% CI | n | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Human-only | 14.7 | 14.4–15.0 | 35 | Above chance, far behind models |
| Automators | 10.4 | 9.7–11.0 | 24 | Better than humans (p < .001), worse than AI-only (5.5) |
| **Validators** | **31.7** | 20.6–42.7 | 9 | **Worse than humans alone** (p = .017) |
| **Cyborgs** | **3.8** | 3.5–4.1 | 9 | Lower error than every individual AI model; ≈/below strongest models |
| AI-only (mean) | 5.5 | — | — | Baseline |
| Market benchmark | 3.5 | — | — | External reference |

Only Cyborgs improved on the machine. Cyborgs matched or exceeded (i.e. lower error than) the market benchmark of 3.5; Automators and Validators did not approach it. The Cyborg vs. pooled AI-only comparison was marginal (p = .06), reflecting the four-model baseline — Cyborg error was below each of the four models individually.

### Human capital predicts hybrid accuracy — benchmarks do not

- Among the four models, AI-only accuracy tracked scale/benchmark standing (GPT-4o and Gemini 3 Pro 4.6; Qwen3 5.5; Llama 3.1 7.1).
- Among **hybrid** forecasters, cognitive ability (the human analog of a benchmark score) did **not** predict accuracy: g r = −0.10, fluid reasoning r = −0.08 (both n.s.).
- **Collaborative human capital did:** perspective-taking r = −0.32 (p = .04); curiosity r = −0.26 and intellectual humility r = −0.25 (trending).
- **Contrast with solo forecasting:** among human-only participants, *every* trait strongly predicted accuracy — intellectual humility r = −0.66 (p < .001); g, fluid reasoning, curiosity, and perspective-taking all near r = −0.45 (p < .01).
- Interpretation: "Access to AI thus decouples raw ability from outcome — the model supplies a floor — while collaborative capacity instead governs who transcends that floor."

### Who becomes a Cyborg

Human capital sharply gated Cyborg emergence. Cyborgs exceeded other hybrids on intellectual humility (6.5 vs. 4.4, d = 4.1), curiosity (6.7 vs. 5.1, d = 4.0), perspective-taking (6.5 vs. 4.6, d = 3.2), with smaller but reliable gaps in fluid reasoning (d = 1.5) and g (d = 1.7), all p < .01. Separation was so strong a logistic model of Cyborg emergence was not identifiable at this sample size — evidence of a very strong association, not a null.

### Qualitative complementarity

Process notes suggest a division of labour: in Cyborg pairs humans drove exploration of the ill-posed, contingent parts of a question (what could intervene, what the market might be missing) while the model anchored the well-posed, data-bound parts. Automators outsourced both; Validators used the model to crystallise, rather than challenge, their own reasoning.

## Discussion

Reframes the productivity question from *whether* AI helps to *when and how*. Pooling all hybrid styles into one average hides a strongly trimodal outcome, including a mode (Validator) that underperforms the AI and the unaided human. The organising moderator is collaborative human capital (perspective-taking, curiosity, intellectual humility) — not the human analog of a model benchmark. Raw cognitive ability predicted solo accuracy and tracked model benchmark ordering but did not predict who succeeded with AI.

**Limitations.** Pilot; Cyborg and Validator cells small (n = 9 each); style is emergent and collinear with human capital (descriptive, not causal); low within-condition variance inflates some standardised effect sizes (interpretation rests on rank-based omnibus and CIs, not d); four-model market baseline; per-question calibration not independently re-audited. A pre-registered, powered replication is in preparation.

## Supplementary notes (incidental)

- **S1 — "Socratic" model that never answers:** a low-tier model re-tuned to withhold direct answers and prompt reasoning failed every accuracy benchmark, yet the **Cyborg rate rose to 30%** (vs. 21% in the main hybrid pool); would-be Automators, unable to copy an answer, fell to human-level accuracy (14.9 vs. 10.4); Cyborgs stayed accurate (4.2). User satisfaction was uniformly poor (CSAT ≈ 1–2 / 10).
- **S2 — EEG:** an opportunistic, unblinded mobile-EEG observation in 7 volunteers found Automators showed ≈43% lower relative gamma power (a contested proxy for cognitive effort) than human-only participants (0.57 vs. 1.0). Reported only to motivate instrumented follow-up.

## Relation to the book

This pilot is well-targeted to **§4.3.4 *Calibrated Trust*** and **§4.3.1 (division of labour)**: it gives an empirical, real-money demonstration that *how* a person collaborates with AI — not the model's benchmark score or the person's raw intelligence — determines whether pairing helps or hurts. The trimodal result is the key argument: deferring wholesale (Automators) leaves value on the table, and rubber-stamping a prior guess (Validators) is actively *worse* than working alone, while genuine complementary reasoning (Cyborgs) beats the model and the market. The dispositions the book cares about — perspective-taking and intellectual humility — are exactly the traits that predicted reaching the productive mode (perspective-taking r = −0.32; humility separating Cyborgs at d = 4.1). The Cyborg division of labour (human owns the ill-posed/contingent, model anchors the data-bound) is a concrete template for §4.3.1. Cite with the pilot caveat.
