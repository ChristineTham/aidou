> **Academic preprint — an arXiv paper, not (as far as can be confirmed) a peer-reviewed publication yet.** It proposes a new inference method and reports the authors' own experiments, so it is a genuine research result rather than opinion, but a single team's first paper on the idea, not established practice. Cite it as promising research, noting the preprint status and that the numbers are the authors' own. Code released at github.com/alexzhang13/rlm.

## Abstract

The paper asks whether a general-purpose LLM's *effective* context can be scaled by orders of magnitude without a bigger window, by spending more compute at inference time. Its answer is the **Recursive Language Model (RLM)**: rather than feeding a very long prompt into the network directly, the prompt is left as a variable inside a REPL (a small programming environment), and the model writes code to peek into it, decompose it, and *recursively call itself* over the snippets it needs. The key claim: "arbitrarily long user prompts should not be fed into the neural network directly but should instead be treated as part of the environment that the LLM is tasked to symbolically and recursively interact with."

## Key findings (the authors' own, on their benchmarks)

- **Beyond the window.** RLMs "can successfully process inputs more than an order of magnitude beyond model context window limits." Figure 1 shows an RLM over GPT-5 holding strong performance from 8K up past 1M tokens on three long-context tasks (S-NIAH, OOLONG, OOLONG-Pairs), while plain GPT-5 degrades steeply with length — the "context rot" phenomenon (Hong et al., 2025) — and cannot even fit inputs past its 272K window.
- **Even on prompts that fit.** Across four diverse long-context tasks, an RLM over GPT-5 beat strong scaffolds at comparable cost, by a median of **26% against compaction, 130% against CodeAct with sub-calls, and 13% against Claude Code**.
- **A post-trained small model.** RLM-Qwen3-8B beat the underlying Qwen3-8B by a median of **28%** and approached vanilla GPT-5 on three long-context tasks.

## Why the mechanism matters

The paper frames RLMs as an *inference-time scaling* strategy, analogous to how reasoning models (chain of thought) added not just gains but theoretical expressive power over vanilla transformers (they cite Merrill & Sabharwal, 2024 — a source the book uses in §1.3). Its critique of the dominant alternative is pointed: *compaction* (repeatedly summarising context once it exceeds a threshold) "is rarely expressive enough for tasks that require dense access throughout the prompt", because it "presumes that some details that appear early in the prompt can safely be forgotten." RLMs instead keep the whole prompt available and let the model fetch into it on demand.

## Relation to the book
