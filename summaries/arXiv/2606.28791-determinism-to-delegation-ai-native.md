# From Determinism to Delegation: AI-Native Software Engineering and the Evolution of the Agentic Engineer

> Source: arXiv:2606.28791 (2026-06-27). Alenezi, M. (Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority, SDAIA). PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2606.28791-determinism-to-delegation-ai-native.pdf`.

## Thesis
This position paper argues that "AI-Native Software Engineering" is a paradigm shift, not an incremental tooling upgrade, that produces a distinct professional archetype—the *Agentic Engineer*—whose primary artifact is the agentic system rather than the program. The shift runs along three axes: the unit of work moves from function to supervised agent workflow; the correctness model moves from binary assertion to statistical evaluation under uncertainty; and the accountability model moves from authorship to outcome ownership. The central claim is *symbiosis, not substitution*: agentic engineering is built upon, and stays accountable through, classical engineering discipline, with disciplined oversight—not raw automation—as the load-bearing competency.

## Key findings
- Controlled evidence is real but heterogeneous and contested. A lab experiment found developers completed a bounded JavaScript task **55.8% faster** with an AI assistant (Peng et al. 2023); three field RCTs across Microsoft, Accenture, and a Fortune 100 firm reported a combined **+26.08% completed tasks** across **4,867 developers**, with the largest gains for *less* experienced developers (Cui et al. 2026) — Table 2, p. 7.
- A METR RCT of **16 experienced** open-source developers on their own mature repos found early-2025 AI tools *increased* task completion time by **19%** (a slowdown), even though the same developers forecast a 20–24% speedup (Becker et al. 2025) — Table 2, p. 7. The gap between perceived and actual gain is itself framed as a managed risk.
- Compositional reliability decays multiplicatively: under independence, end-to-end success is `P = p^n`, so a strong per-step rate of **p = 0.95 yields only ~0.36 over 20 steps** — motivating trajectory-level evaluation and human checkpoints over per-step accuracy (Eq. 5, Fig. 2, pp. 5–6).
- Agentic security is a distinct threat class: indirect prompt injection successfully attacked ReAct-prompted GPT-4 agents in roughly **24% of cases** (INJECAGENT, Zhan et al.) — p. 8. Defenses differ in kind from perimeter security: tool-permission scoping, output guardrails, sandboxing, and HITL gates.
- LLM-as-judge grading is itself biased (position, verbosity, self-preference) and judge choice can reorder model rankings, introducing a circularity that calibration and ensembling only partially mitigate — p. 7.
- The role is mapped onto SFIA 9 (Levels 1–7) and to ISO/IEC 42001, IEEE 7000, and NIST AI RMF governance standards; six falsifiable predictions (H1–H6) are stated with refutation conditions — pp. 8–9.

## Relevance to the book
This is exactly the peer-reviewed, balance-providing counterweight Chapter 3 needs against a single opinion writer (Ahuja). It supplies numbers on *both* sides of the productivity debate (double-digit field gains vs. an expert-developer slowdown), grounds the vibe-coding-vs-spec-driven tension in a formal correctness/accountability framework, and reframes the value migration as spec-writing, evaluation, and outcome ownership—directly on-topic for intent-driven development (ICE) and AI-native software engineering. It is explicit that its comparison table and forecasts are analytical constructs and hypotheses, not validated instruments, which the chapter can cite honestly.

## Stance
**AI-native-framing** (with an empirical-vibe and quality-skeptic streak). It advances a paradigm-shift thesis while deliberately foregrounding disconfirming evidence—the METR expert slowdown, compositional-reliability decay, LLM-judge bias, and prompt-injection attack rates—to argue that oversight and governance, not automation, are the durable competencies.
