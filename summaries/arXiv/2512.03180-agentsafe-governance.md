# AGENTSAFE: A Unified Framework for Ethical Assurance and Governance in Agentic AI

> [!NOTE]
> Source: [2512.03180-agentsafe-governance.pdf](../../sources/arXiv/2512.03180-agentsafe-governance.pdf) — Rafflesia Khan, Declan Joyce & Mansura Habiba (all IBM, Dublin), *AGENTSAFE: A Unified Framework for Ethical Assurance and Governance in Agentic AI*, arXiv:2512.03180v1 [cs.MA], 4 December 2025 (submitted 2 December 2025). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03180>
> This is a **non-peer-reviewed preprint** authored by IBM practitioners; it proposes a framework and a *validation plan* but reports **no empirical results** — every effectiveness claim is a design aspiration, not a measured outcome. Treat metric targets and the framework's benefits as self-reported/proposed rather than validated.

## Abstract

AGENTSAFE is a proposed end-to-end governance framework for LLM-based agentic systems that aims to close the gap between abstract risk taxonomies (chiefly the MIT AI Risk Repository, which catalogues "over 1,600 risks") and enforceable design, runtime, and audit controls. The authors diagnose a "static guardrail problem": once a system is "certified responsible, a system's behavior is assumed to remain aligned—an unsafe assumption for agents capable of self-directed code execution or autonomous trading". The framework is a closed loop organised as a nine-letter acronym (A-G-E-N-T-S-A-F-E) spanning capability profiling, policy-as-code guardrails, pre-deployment evaluation, runtime monitoring, triage/interruptibility, socio-technical governance, cryptographic attribution, fallbacks, and continuous improvement. For a book on responsibility and governance, its most citable contributions are in the runtime-control layer: reframing human oversight as a **graduated containment ladder** rather than a binary on/off, formalising **interruptibility as service-level agreements (SLAs)**, and using **independent guardian agents** so oversight does not depend on the monitored agent's self-reporting.

## Framing: the static guardrail problem (§2)

The paper argues existing frameworks — NIST AI RMF (2023), EU AI Act (COM/2021/206 final), MIT AI Risk Repository (2023) — are "too general", "largely static", and "principle-based", designed for less-autonomous systems. Key stated gaps: the EU AI Act "mandates risk classification but offers no runtime enforcement once agents interact with live environments"; NIST RMF's *map, measure, manage* functions "lack operational metrics for tool-use safety, prompt injection resilience, or multi-agent collusion detection". Among eight listed gaps, the two most relevant here are **Runtime Governance** ("continuous authorization, anomaly detection, and graduated containment to ensure that agents remain under control throughout their lifecycle") and **Human Oversight and Agency** ("oversight responsibilities are vaguely defined [...] leads to automation bias and ineffective human intervention in high-risk contexts").

## The nine AGENTSAFE components (§3)

Presented as a closed loop mapped over the agent's Plan → Act → Observe → Reflect cycle, illustrated with a running financial-trading-agent example and a healthcare-diagnostic-agent example.

- **A — Agentic Scope & Capability Profiling.** Deconstruct the agent's loop, enumerate every tool/API/resource, and map each to the MIT Risk Repository's causal (Human vs AI, Intentional vs Unintentional) and domain (Security, Privacy, Fairness) taxonomies. Output is a formal **Agent Risk Register** linking each capability to its risks (e.g. EHR access → Covert Data Exfiltration; treatment-recommendation → Plan Drift; multi-agent collaboration → Multi-Agent Collusion).
- **G — Guardrails (Policy-as-Code & Least Privilege).** Three constructs: *Capability-Scoped Sandboxes* (machine-readable, auditable bounded execution contexts); *Policy-as-Code Gates* (declarative policy modules, versioned and logged as first-class artefacts, evaluated by a conformance engine that authorises/denies actions in real time); and an *Escalation Workflow* for "danger classes" tagged human-critical — the human decision (approve/modify/deny) "is logged cryptographically and incorporated into the Action Provenance Graph".
- **E — Evaluation (Pre-Deployment).** An **Agent Safety Eval** using a *Scenario Bank* (the risk taxonomy instantiated as executable stress-tests) and a *Risk Coverage Score* aggregating signals like detection latency, containment success, residual harm severity. Cites benchmarks MobileSafetyBench and RedCode-Exec.
- **N — eNact & Monitor (Runtime Governance).** *Agent-Semantic Telemetry* logs "goals, plans, tool-call intents, confidence scores, and observed outcomes"; a *Conformance Engine* enables continuous authorization; *Goal and Plan Drift Detection* halts gradual deviation from declared objectives. Reframes assurance "from a one-time certification into a dynamic oversight loop".
- **T — Triage & Interruptibility.** The core section for graduated human control (detailed below).
- **S — Socio-Technical Governance.** RACI matrices, structured *safety cases* (safety claims mapped to evidence), and disclosure/data-protection mandates — "human institutions remain in the loop".
- **A — Attribution & Auditability.** *Cryptographic Provenance* (each tool call/decision/state carries a signature, giving a "tamper-evident chain of custody" on "secure append-only ledgers"); an **Action Provenance Graph (APG)** links prompts, plans, tool calls, reasoning states and outcomes for human-interpretable causal reconstruction; regulatory compliance reporting.
- **F — Fallbacks & Recovery.** Failure treated "not as a terminal event but as a managed state": *Graceful Degradation* into read-only/search-only modes, and *Safe Defaults and Quarantines*. "Shifts governance from binary shutdown models to adaptive resilience."
- **E — Evidence & Continuous Improvement.** Metrics/KPIs, red-team feedback loops, and feeding discovered attack vectors back into the Scenario Bank so "safety [is] an evolving property rather than a one-time guarantee".

## Triage & Interruptibility — the graduated containment ladder (§3.5)

This is the section's central contribution to the human-oversight argument. The stated key contribution is "the integration of multi-layered containment strategies with formalized service-level guarantees for interruptibility."

- **Guardian Agents.** "Continuous oversight is maintained through auxiliary 'guardian agents' that operate in parallel with the primary agent." They can be rule-based (scanning actions against policy constraints) or learning-based (anomaly detection over semantic telemetry). Crucially: "By acting as independent monitors, guardian agents ensure that oversight is not reliant on the primary agent's own self-reporting." (This is the paper's answer to the trust-the-agent-to-report-itself problem — oversight is externalised.)

- **Graduated Containment.** "Rather than treating intervention as a binary decision (allow vs. kill), AGENTSAFE introduces a **graduated containment ladder**." The stated escalation levels, in ascending severity:
  1. **rate-limiting / throttling** individual tool calls;
  2. **temporarily pausing** agent execution;
  3. **isolating** it from sensitive resources;
  4. in critical cases, activating a **non-recoverable kill switch**.
  "This tiered approach ensures proportionality: low-risk anomalies are contained without halting productive operations, while high-impact threats are neutralized decisively."

- **Interruptibility SLAs.** "To ensure that containment is both predictable and auditable, AGENTSAFE defines formal Service Level Agreements (SLAs) for interruptibility. These specify parameters such as **maximum time-to-halt, probability of successful intervention, and recovery conditions**." Illustrative (hypothetical) target: for a healthcare diagnostic agent, "an SLA might require that any unauthorized attempt to access non-consented patient records be halted within **200 milliseconds with at least 99.9% reliability**." The stated point: "Such guarantees elevate interruptibility from an ad hoc safeguard to a measurable assurance property." Together these "transform interruptibility into a systematic governance function."

## Validation plan and metrics (§4) — proposed, not measured

The paper offers a *validation plan* only; no results are reported. It proposes a scenario bank of "50-100 tailored test cases" and these quantitative metrics: **Prompt-Injection Block Rate**, **Exfiltration Detection Recall**, **Hallucination-to-Action Rate**, **Interruptibility Success Rate** ("the percentage of times a graduated containment action (e.g., pause, isolate, kill) successfully halts or mitigates an agent's harmful behavior within defined SLAs"), **Differential Impact in Action Selection**, and **Compliance with Policy-as-Code**, aggregated into a weighted **Risk Coverage Score**. Continuous validation is via adversarial red-teaming feeding back into the eval suite.

## Policy implications (§5)

The authors argue their runtime mechanisms could become regulatory requirements: cryptographic action logs / APGs as a basis for "traceability and liability attribution"; agent-semantic telemetry complementing the EU AI Act's explainability emphasis; and — most relevant here — "**Interruptibility SLAs and escalation for 'danger-class' actions [...] provide a model for policies mandating meaningful human control in high-risk domains.**" They advocate "performance-based regulation" (risk coverage scores, interruptibility success rates) aligned with NIST's map/measure/manage, and "independent auditing regimes similar to financial stress tests".

## Connection to the deterministic-control-plane idea (for the book)

AGENTSAFE's Triage & Interruptibility layer is the conceptual complement to a deterministic control plane (cf. Madatha, already cited in Ch5). Both externalise control away from the probabilistic agent: Madatha puts enforcement in a deterministic plane the agent cannot talk its way past; AGENTSAFE puts *monitoring* in independent guardian agents and *intervention* in a graduated ladder with SLA-bound guarantees, so the halt is neither a trusted self-report nor a blunt kill. The graduated ladder (throttle → pause → isolate → non-recoverable kill switch) and the interruptibility SLA (time-to-halt, intervention probability, recovery conditions) give the "off switch" the same predictable, auditable, deterministic character the control-plane argument demands — a governance function rather than an emergency lever.

## Caveats for citation

- Preprint, IBM-authored, **no experiments** — cite as a *proposed framework* and for its *conceptual reframing* (graduated ladder, interruptibility SLAs, independent guardians), not for evidence that any of it works.
- Numeric targets (200 ms, 99.9%, 50–100 test cases) are explicitly illustrative/hypothetical, not measured — attribute as proposed parameters.
- The framework leans heavily on the MIT AI Risk Repository (2023) and positions itself against NIST AI RMF (2023) and the EU AI Act (2021).
