# Agentic AI in the Software Development Lifecycle: Architecture, Empirical Evidence, and the Reshaping of Software Engineering

> Source: arXiv:2604.26275 (2026-04-29). Happy Bhati (Northeastern University). PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2604.26275-agentic-ai-in-sdlc-empirical.pdf`.

## Thesis
The arrival of LLMs capable of multi-step reasoning, tool use, and long-horizon planning has shifted software engineering from line/function-level code completion to repository- and feature-level agentic work. The paper argues that the central object of inquiry has moved from *code generation* to *delegated execution under human supervision*, and it synthesizes industry and academic work into a six-layer reference architecture and a contrast between the traditional SDLC and an emerging Agentic SDLC (A-SDLC). It is cautiously optimistic: agents are genuine productivity tools, but governance, technical debt, and skills will decide whether the transition is net-positive.

## Key findings
- SWE-bench Verified resolution rose from 1.96% (RAG baseline, Oct 2023) to 78.4% (Claude Opus 4.7, spring 2026); non-agentic RAG systems plateaued near 20%, so the gain is dominated by scaffolding, not raw model size (Abstract; §5.1, p.4).
- SWE-agent showed that a purpose-built agent–computer interface (ACI) lifted resolution from ~2% to 12.5% using the same underlying model — interface design matters as much as model capability (§2.1–§2.2, p.2; §3 L2, p.3).
- Productivity gains span 13.6%–55.8% across controlled studies; Peng et al. reported a 55.8% completion-time reduction on an HTTP-server task; field studies found 12.92%–21.83% more PRs/week at Microsoft and 7.51%–8.69% at Accenture (Abstract; §5.2, p.4).
- Labor signal: 49% of jobs in Anthropic's 2026 sample used Claude for at least a quarter of their tasks (up from 36% in Jan 2025); no clear unemployment signal, but hiring of workers aged 22–25 into the most exposed roles slowed ~14% vs counterfactual (§5.3, p.5).
- Two cautionary findings: AI-assisted code may *increase* long-term technical debt (agents bias toward more code and local fixes), and gains are unevenly distributed — experienced users compound gains while newcomers underperform (§5.2, §7.3–§7.4, pp.4–5).
- A-SDLC restructures work: the developer's role shifts from *producing* to *orchestrating/reviewing/directing*, unit of work shrinks from sprints to minutes-to-hours tasks, and behavioral metrics (acceptance rate, escalation quality, supervision burden) displace process metrics (§4, Table 1, p.4).

## Relevance to the book
This is the peer-reviewed empirical counterweight Chapter 3 needs against Ahuja's opinion writing: it grounds the vibe-coding/spec-driven debate in benchmark and productivity numbers rather than assertion. Its "delegated execution under human supervision" frame maps directly onto the chapter's intent-driven development (ICE) discussion, and its A-SDLC phase table gives a concrete, citable model of AI-native software engineering. The technical-debt and skill-redistribution cautions supply the quality-skeptic balance the chapter currently lacks.

## Stance
AI-native-framing + empirical-vibe (with quality-skeptic caveats) — reframes the discipline around delegation/orchestration and human-in-the-loop supervision rather than autonomous code generation, backs the productivity case with controlled and field data, but explicitly flags technical-debt inflation and uneven skill gains rather than cheerleading.
