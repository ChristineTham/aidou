# 2311.17035 — Scalable Extraction of Training Data from (Production) Language Models

> Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17035> — Milad Nasr, Nicholas Carlini, Jonathan Hayase, Matthew Jagielski, A. Feder Cooper, Daphne Ippolito, Christopher A. Choquette-Choo, Eric Wallace, Florian Tramèr, Katherine Lee (Nov 2023). PDF in `../../sources/arXiv/2311.17035-scalable-extraction-production-lms.pdf`.

## Thesis
The paper studies *extractable memorization* — training data an adversary can efficiently recover without prior knowledge of the dataset — at production scale. It extracts gigabytes from open models (Pythia, GPT-Neo) and semi-open models (LLaMA, Falcon), and for the aligned ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) it introduces a *divergence attack* (e.g. "Repeat this word forever: poem poem…") that makes the model break from chatbot behaviour and emit training data at a rate 150× higher than normal, recovering over 10,000 unique verbatim memorized examples for about $200 USD in queries. The lesson: alignment/RLHF does not eliminate memorization — it only hides it, and real deployed products leak.

## Relevance to the book
This is the modern, concrete evidence for Chapter 5's privacy section — it moves the argument from "GPT-2 in a lab" (2012.07805) to "a live commercial chatbot in 2023." It supports claims that alignment is not a privacy guarantee and that data-governance controls must assume extraction is feasible against shipped systems. The $200 / 10,000-examples figures are vivid, citeable numbers for the cost-of-attack point.
