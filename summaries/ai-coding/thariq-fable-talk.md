## Abstract

The talk is the presented version of Shihipar's "Field Guide to Fable" — how to work with the newest, most capable class of Claude models. His framing image: the model is like an RPG where "the map is opening up" — you leave the tutorial and the open world begins, exciting but intimidating because there is so much you can do. The guide is built in four parts around discovering your *unknowns* — the gap between what you can specify and what the task actually needs — and using the model to surface them (the same known/unknown quadrants, "blind spot pass", "interview me", brainstorming with prototypes, and end-of-session quizzes that appear in the written article).

## What the talk adds beyond the article

Mostly it restates the written guide, but two notes are worth recording:

- **A sharper closing thesis:** "building is easier, but generating value is still hard." Shihipar warns that AI engineers over-focus on the *process* of building and their setups, when the point is to generate value — and "it takes a lot of swings, a lot of tries, to find the valuable stuff." This is a crisp practitioner statement of the book's §6.6 argument (measure value, not output; the question that counts is whether a thing is worth building) — but §6.6 already makes that case with cited sources, so this is at most a light echo, not a new point needing its own citation.
- **A personal frame** for productivity ("work less, spend more time with people I care about"), and a live example: he built the talk's slide deck with Fable in about four hours.
