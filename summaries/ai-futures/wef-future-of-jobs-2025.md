# Future of Jobs Report 2025

> [!NOTE]
> Source: [wef-future-of-jobs-2025.pdf](../../sources/ai-futures/wef-future-of-jobs-2025.pdf) — World Economic Forum, *Future of Jobs Report 2025*, WEF Insight Report, January 2025. https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/
>
> **This is an employer-expectation survey, not a record of measured outcomes.** Every headline figure is a *projection extrapolated from what employers say they expect* over 2025–2030. It draws on the WEF Future of Jobs Survey, conducted in late 2024, which gathered the views of more than 1,000 global employers collectively employing more than 14.1 million workers across 22 industry clusters and 55 economies. The job-creation/displacement totals combine these employer expectations with ILO employment data for a subset of roles (~1.18 billion formal jobs), which the report itself warns "should not be treated as comprehensive." Treat all numbers below as employer projections/expectations rather than observed effects.

## Abstract

The *Future of Jobs Report 2025* is the World Economic Forum's biennial survey of how leading global employers expect the labour market to change by 2030 under five macrotrends: technological change, the green transition, geoeconomic fragmentation, economic uncertainty, and demographic shifts. Its central projection is that structural labour-market transformation will churn the equivalent of **22% of today's jobs** by 2030 — creating **170 million new jobs** (14% of today's total employment) while displacing **92 million** (8%), for a **net gain of 78 million jobs (7%)**. The single most citable AI figure: **86% of surveyed employers expect AI and information processing technologies to transform their business by 2030**, making it the most-cited technology trend and, alongside big data, the top fastest-growing skill. In the report's own words: "on current trends over the 2025 to 2030 period job creation and destruction due to structural labour-market transformation will amount to 22% of today's total jobs." These are employer expectations, and they matter for a chapter on AI's future because they are the most-cited single source for the "AI will create more jobs than it destroys, but churn many" narrative — and because they are projections, not measured outcomes.

## What the report is and how it was produced

Based on the WEF Future of Jobs Survey conducted in late 2024. Respondents: **more than 1,000 global employers**, collectively employing **more than 14.1 million workers**, across **22 industry clusters** and **55 economies**. The report combines employer growth/decline expectations with ILO employment data (ILOSTAT) to estimate absolute job numbers. It covers the 2025–2030 timeframe and is organised into five chapters: macrotrends, jobs outlook, skills outlook, workforce strategies, and region/economy/industry profiles.

The report frames its context: global growth projected at **3.2% for 2025** (IMF), global inflation projected to ease to **3.5%** by end-2025, global unemployment at **4.9%** (lowest since 1991), global youth unemployment elevated at **13%**, and the ILO "jobs gap" standing at **402 million** additional jobs needed in 2024.

## Headline churn projection (employer expectations + ILO data)

- **Structural labour-market churn: 22% of today's total (formal) jobs** over 2025–2030.
- **New jobs created: 170 million** — equivalent to **14%** of today's total employment.
- **Jobs displaced: 92 million** — equivalent to **8%** of today's employment.
- **Net growth: 78 million jobs**, equivalent to **7%** of today's employment.
- The dataset underlying the absolute numbers covers ~**1.18 billion** formal jobs (a subset of ILO's total employment data). The report explicitly cautions the conclusions "should not be treated as comprehensive, but rather as providing insights on selected segments of the global workforce."

## Macrotrends driving business transformation (share of employers)

The report's Figure 1.1 ranks the macrotrends employers expect to transform their business by 2030:

- Broadening digital access — **60%** (most transformative trend overall)
- Rising cost of living / inflation — **50%** (second overall)
- Increased efforts to reduce carbon emissions — **47%** (third overall)
- Increased focus on labour and social issues — **46%**
- Slower economic growth — **42%**
- Increased efforts to adapt to climate change — **41%**
- Ageing and declining working-age populations — **40%**
- Increased geopolitical division and conflicts — **34%**
- Growing working-age populations — **25%** (also cited as 24% in Figure 1.1)
- Increased restrictions to global trade and investment — **23%**
- Increased government subsidies and industrial policy — **21%**
- Stricter anti-trust and competition regulations — **17%**

### Technology trends (Figure 1.2, share of employers expecting transformation)

- **AI and information processing technologies — 86%** (the single biggest expected driver of any technology)
- Robots and autonomous systems — **58%**
- Energy generation, storage and distribution — **41%**
- New materials and composites — **30%**
- Semiconductors and computing — **20%**
- Sensing, laser and optical — **18%**
- Quantum and encryption — **12%**
- Biotechnology and gene technologies — **11%**
- Satellites and space technologies — **9%**

The report notes that since the release of ChatGPT in November 2022, investment flows into AI have increased "nearly eightfold." It cites workplace studies showing generative AI can enhance human skills and substitute for tacit knowledge among newer/average performers, but "risks producing adverse outcomes where users unknowingly stretch it beyond its capability," and warns that without appropriate frameworks and possibly regulation, development could focus on replacing rather than augmenting human work, "which could increase inequality and unemployment."

## Fastest-growing and fastest-declining roles (employer projections)

**Fastest-growing in percentage terms** (top of Figure 2.2): Big Data Specialists, FinTech Engineers, AI and Machine Learning Specialists, Software and Applications Developers, Security Management Specialists, Data Warehousing Specialists, Autonomous and Electric Vehicle Specialists, UI/UX Designers, Light Truck/Delivery Drivers, Internet of Things Specialists, Data Analysts and Scientists, Environmental Engineers, Information Security Analysts, DevOps Engineers, Renewable Energy Engineers. Technology roles lead; green/energy-transition roles also feature prominently.

**Fastest-declining in percentage terms**: Data Entry Clerks, Bank Tellers and Related Clerks, Material-Recording and Stock-Keeping Clerks, Door-to-Door Sales/Street Vendors, Administrative Assistants and Executive Secretaries, Legal Secretaries, Printing Workers, Legal Officials, Postal Service Clerks, Telemarketers, Accounting/Bookkeeping/Payroll Clerks, Cashiers and Ticket Clerks, Graphic Designers, Claims Adjusters. Primary drivers cited: broadening digital access, AI and information processing, and robots and autonomous systems.

**Largest growth in absolute terms** (Figure 2.4): Frontline/core-economy roles dominate — Farmworkers (projected **+35 million** jobs, the single largest, currently >200 million workers), Delivery Drivers, Building Construction Workers, Salespersons, Food Processing Workers. Care-economy roles (Nursing Professionals, Social Work and Counselling Professionals, Personal Care Aides) and education roles (Higher/Secondary Education Teachers) also grow significantly. **Largest absolute decline**: Clerical and Secretarial Workers, including Cashiers and Ticket Clerks and Administrative Assistants/Executive Secretaries.

### Macrotrend-level job effects (Figure 2.6, employer attribution)

- Broadening digital access: expected to **create 19M and displace 9M** (largest of any single trend on both sides).
- AI and information processing technologies: expected to **create 11M and displace 9M** — the largest job-displacing technology trend and a net job creator.
- Robots and autonomous systems: expected **net decline of ~5 million** jobs (largest net displacer, ~4.8M).
- Slower economic growth: expected to displace ~1.6 million jobs.

## Skills outlook and reskilling projection

- On average, employers expect **39% of workers' existing skill sets will be transformed or become outdated** over 2025–2030 ("skill instability"). This has *slowed* from 44% in the 2023 edition and a 57% peak in 2020 (post-pandemic). The report attributes the slowdown partly to a rising share of workers — **50%** — having completed training/reskilling/upskilling, up from **41%** in 2023.
- **Reskilling projection: "if the world's workforce was made up of 100 people, 59 would need training by 2030."** Of these 100: **29** could be upskilled in their current roles, **19** upskilled and redeployed elsewhere in their organisation, and **11 would be unlikely to receive the reskilling or upskilling needed** — leaving their employment prospects at risk. (Equivalently, ~59% of workers need training by 2030.)
- **Fastest-growing skills**: AI and big data top the list, followed by networks and cybersecurity, then technological literacy. Also rising: creative thinking; resilience, flexibility and agility; curiosity and lifelong learning; and — for the first time in the top 10 — environmental stewardship.
- **Most sought-after core skill in 2025**: analytical thinking (essential for 7 in 10 companies), then resilience/flexibility/agility, then leadership and social influence.
- **Declining skills**: manual dexterity, endurance and precision (24% of respondents foresee a decrease).
- **Skill gaps are named the biggest barrier to transformation**, cited by **63%** of employers.

## Workforce strategies employers plan (self-reported intentions)

- **85%** plan to prioritise upskilling their workforce.
- **70%** expect to hire staff with new skills.
- **50%** plan to transition staff from declining to growing roles.
- **40%** plan to reduce staff as their skills become less relevant.
- **64%** identify supporting employee health and well-being as a key talent-attraction strategy.
- On AI specifically: **half of employers plan to re-orient their business in response to AI**, **two-thirds plan to hire talent with specific AI skills**, and **40% anticipate reducing their workforce where AI can automate tasks.**
- **52%** anticipate allocating a greater share of revenue to wages by 2030 (only 8% expect a decline).
- Diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives reported by **83%** of employers (up from 67% in 2023).

## Stated limitations

The report is explicit that its figures are extrapolations from employer expectations combined with ILO data; that its absolute job numbers cover only a ~1.18 billion-job subset and "should not be treated as comprehensive"; and that the long-term productivity gains from generative AI "remain uncertain." Skill-instability and reskilling shares are survey-derived expectations, not measured outcomes.
