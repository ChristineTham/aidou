# vaccaro-2024-human-ai-combinations — When combinations of humans and AI are useful: a systematic review and meta-analysis

> Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1 — Michelle Vaccaro, Abdullah Almaatouq & Thomas Malone. *Nature Human Behaviour* 8(12), 2293–2303 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1. (Journal article — no PDF downloaded.)

## Thesis
A preregistered systematic review and meta-analysis of 106 experimental studies (370 effect sizes, published Jan 2020–Jun 2023) found that, on average, human–AI combinations performed *significantly worse* than the better of humans-alone or AI-alone (Hedges' g = −0.23; 95% CI −0.39 to −0.07). The losses were concentrated in decision-making tasks, whereas content-creation tasks showed gains; and combinations improved on the baseline mainly when the human alone already outperformed the AI, but declined when the AI alone was stronger. The lesson: "centaur" synergy is not automatic — on the whole it is the exception, and pairing a person with a superior AI often drags results below the AI's solo performance.

## Relevance to the book
This is the key counterweight for Ch6's complementarity/appropriate-reliance section. It resists the optimistic "human + AI always beats either alone" framing and supplies a hard, quantified baseline (average g = −0.23) showing complementarity must be engineered, not assumed. It sets up the conditions-for-reliance discussion: gains appear in generative/creative work, losses in high-stakes decisions, and the human's own competence relative to the AI predicts whether the team helps or hurts.
