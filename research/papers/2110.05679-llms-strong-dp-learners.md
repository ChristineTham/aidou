# 2110.05679 — Large Language Models Can Be Strong Differentially Private Learners

> Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05679> — Xuechen Li, Florian Tramèr, Percy Liang, Tatsunori Hashimoto (Oct 2021; ICLR 2022). PDF in `pdf/2110.05679.pdf`.

## Thesis
This paper is the mitigation counterweight: it shows the large performance drop usually seen when applying differentially private SGD (DP-SGD) to NLP can be largely closed by (1) fine-tuning large pretrained models, (2) DP-tuned hyperparameters, and (3) pretraining-aligned objectives — yielding models that beat prior state-of-the-art DP-trained models under the same privacy budget (ε). It also contributes a memory-saving "ghost clipping" technique that runs DP-SGD "with almost the same memory cost as non-private training," and finds, contrary to conventional wisdom, that private fine-tuning of pretrained LMs does not suffer dimension-dependent degradation. The lesson: strong formal privacy guarantees for LLMs are practically achievable, so memorization/extraction risk can be actively mitigated rather than merely accepted.

## Relevance to the book
This balances the extraction papers (2012.07805, 2311.17035) in Chapter 5's data-governance discussion. Where those establish the risk, this establishes that there is a principled defence — differential privacy — with a real (if imperfect) utility cost. It supports the governance argument that organisations training or fine-tuning on sensitive data have concrete options (DP-SGD) rather than only procedural controls, and sets up the tension that DP still trades off accuracy, especially on rare/long-tailed data.
