# doshi-hauser-2024 — Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content

> Source: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290 — Anil R. Doshi, Oliver P. Hauser (2024). *Science Advances*, 10(28), eadn5290. (Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/)

## Thesis

In an online experiment where 293 writers produced short stories with 0, 1, or 5 generative-AI story ideas and 600 evaluators rated them, access to AI ideas raised individual story novelty by 5.4% (one idea) to 8.1% (five ideas) and usefulness by 3.7% to 9.0%, with the largest gains (10–11%) accruing to the least creative writers. But AI-assisted stories were measurably more similar to one another than human-only stories — a homogenization amounting to roughly 9–11% of the observed range — and drifted toward the AI's own suggestions. The result is a social dilemma: each writer is individually better off, yet the collective pool of creative output narrows.

## Relevance to the book

Core source for Ch6 cluster (c) on creativity and homogenization, with clean, citeable numbers on both sides of the trade-off. Supports the chapter's argument that AI can lift the individual floor while flattening collective diversity, framing homogenization as an emergent, aggregate risk rather than a per-user one. Balance point: it does not say AI kills creativity — it enhances it individually — which lets Ch6 present a genuine tension instead of a doom narrative.
