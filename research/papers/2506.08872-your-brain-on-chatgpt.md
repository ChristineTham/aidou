# 2506.08872 — Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task

> Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872 — Nataliya Kosmyna, Eugene Hauptmann, Ye Tong Yuan, Jessica Situ, Xian-Hao Liao, Ashly Vivian Beresnitzky, Iris Braunstein, Pattie Maes (2025, MIT Media Lab). PDF in `pdf/2506.08872.pdf`.

## Thesis

An EEG study of 54 participants writing essays across three sessions in one of three conditions — LLM (ChatGPT), Search Engine, or Brain-only (no tools) — found that brain connectivity scaled down with the amount of external support: Brain-only participants showed the strongest, most distributed EEG networks, Search Engine users moderate, and LLM users the weakest neural coupling. In a fourth session (n=18) where LLM users were reassigned to Brain-only, they showed under-engagement and weaker connectivity than participants who had never used the LLM, and over 83% of LLM users could not quote a single sentence from the essay they had just written. The authors term the effect "cognitive debt": AI assistance boosts fluency and speed while eroding mental effort, memory encoding, and sense of ownership.

## Relevance to the book

Direct primary evidence for Ch6 cluster (a) on cognitive offloading and critical thinking — the flagship EEG "cognitive debt" result. It supports the chapter's caution that AI-assisted production can hollow out the learning and retention that the work is supposed to build, and pairs with the behavioural Gerlich (2025) finding. Balance note: this is a small (n=54), non-peer-reviewed preprint that the authors themselves flag as preliminary; cite it as suggestive neuroscience, not settled fact.
