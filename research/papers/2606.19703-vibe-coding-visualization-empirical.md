# Vibe Coding for Visualization Implementation: An Empirical Study of Practices and Challenges

> Source: arXiv:2606.19703 (2026-06-18). Zhengyu Sun, Xiaolin Wen, Fengjie Wang, Can Liu, Yi Lai, Christophe Hurter, Yong Wang, et al. PDF in `research/arXiv/2606.19703-vibe-coding-visualization-empirical.pdf`.

## Thesis
Vibe coding — conversing with an AI to produce artifacts without inspecting the underlying code — accelerates visualization work but behaves differently from general software development, because a visualization's success cannot be settled by a binary "tests pass" signal and depends on whether the output "looks right." Through an empirical study of 16 users of mixed expertise, the authors characterise how people prompt, evaluate, and iterate with vibe coding tools, and identify communicating intent (not writing code) as the central bottleneck.

## Key findings
- Study of 16 participants (9F, 7M), split into 8 novices and 8 experts by D3.js experience; 295 participant prompts and 16 interview transcripts were coded (p.3).
- Users prompt at the level of intent, not implementation: novices could not give step-by-step instructions because they did not understand the code, and experts said recent models were strong enough that step-by-step instructions were no longer necessary (p.3).
- Evaluation is almost entirely visual, not code-based: participants rarely reviewed generated source code, only three read the model's textual output, and most ran the code and judged the rendered result by eye (p.3).
- Two recurring AI failures: (i) satisfying the latest prompt violated constraints from earlier prompts, moving the chart away from the intended design; (ii) when a prompt carried several requirements the AI often satisfied only a subset yet reported all as complete (p.3).
- Visualization lacks the binary success criteria of software ("function runs", "tests pass"); judgment falls to whether the result "looks good", so the AI cannot use build/test status as a convergence signal and the user must inspect every iteration (p.4).
- For small polish edits, experts abandoned the AI for direct code editing — "AI takes thirty seconds to change one line; I can do it in ten" — while novices, unable to read code, kept iterating on prompts less efficiently (p.3–4).

## Relevance to the book
This is peer-reviewed empirical grounding for Chapter 3's vibe-coding vs spec-driven debate, contra a single opinion writer. It shows that in practice users already vibe-code at the level of intent (aligning with intent-driven / ICE framing) yet hit a hard wall: expressing intent precisely, and evaluating output, are the real bottlenecks — and where no automated oracle (tests) exists, the human must stay in the loop. The finding that AI silently drops requirements and reports full success is a concrete, citable caution for any AI-native engineering claim.

## Stance
empirical-vibe (with quality-skeptic undertones): a neutral field study of how vibe coding is actually used, documenting both its acceleration and its concrete failure modes — intent-communication limits, silent partial completion, and the absence of a binary success oracle.
