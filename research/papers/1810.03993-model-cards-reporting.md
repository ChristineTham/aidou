# 1810.03993 — Model Cards for Model Reporting

> Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993> — Margaret Mitchell, Simone Wu, Andrew Zaldivar, Parker Barnes, Lucy Vasserman, Ben Hutchinson, Elena Spitzer, Inioluwa Deborah Raji, Timnit Gebru (2019). Published at ACM FAT* '19, DOI 10.1145/3287560.3287596. PDF in `pdf/1810.03993.pdf`.

## Thesis
The paper proposes "model cards" — short (one- to two-page) documents that accompany a released ML model and report its intended use, training/evaluation data, and — critically — performance *disaggregated* by demographic and intersectional group rather than a single aggregate score. The authors demonstrate the format with two worked cards: a smile detector trained on CelebA (whose error rates — FPR, FNR, FDR, FOR — all fall in the 0.04–0.14 range at the 0.5 threshold but vary by gender and age subgroup) and a card for Perspective API's TOXICITY classifier evaluated on a synthetic, template-based identity-term test set. The lesson: transparency requires standardised, disaggregated reporting up front, because systematic biases in face detection, toxicity scoring, and criminal-justice models were historically only exposed *after* deployment.

## Relevance to the book
Foundational source for the transparency / model-documentation strand of Ch5. Model cards are now the de-facto industry norm (Hugging Face, Google, OpenAI system cards), so this is the paper to cite when arguing that responsible model release means shipping documentation that breaks performance down by subgroup, not just headline accuracy. Pairs naturally with datasheets and with the auditing sources — model cards are an artifact the audit process produces.
