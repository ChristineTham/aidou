# 2402.07632 — Understanding the Effects of Miscalibrated AI Confidence on User Trust, Reliance, and Decision Efficacy

> Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07632 — Jingshu Li, Yitian Yang, Renwen Zhang, Q. Vera Liao, Tianqi Song, Zhengtao Xu, Yi-chieh Lee (2024). PDF in `pdf/2402.07632.pdf`.

## Thesis
Two controlled experiments (N = 126 each, 42 per condition) on a three-city image-recognition task (65.1% unaided baseline) show that when an AI's stated confidence is *miscalibrated*, users cannot detect it (only ~26–29% correctly identified over- or under-confident AI vs 76% for well-calibrated) and their reliance goes wrong: overconfident AI drove over-reliance to 41.3% (vs 28.2% calibrated) while underconfident AI drove under-reliance to 17.7% (vs 11.2%), and only the well-calibrated AI produced the best decision-accuracy gain (+11.9% vs ~+6.5–7.2%). Disclosing the calibration status (Exp 2) helped users spot the problem but backfired: it cut trust and switching without improving accuracy (no significant accuracy change, all p > 0.6). The lesson: appropriate reliance depends on well-calibrated AI confidence, and simply telling users the AI is unreliable does not repair the team.

## Relevance to the book
Supports Ch6's appropriate-reliance / over- vs under-reliance material with a concrete mechanism and numbers: reliance is a behaviour that miscalibration distorts in *both* directions, and a naive transparency fix (surface the calibration) does not restore complementarity. Pairs with Vaccaro et al. (2024) — it explains one reason human–AI teams underperform, and cautions the book against treating "just be transparent about confidence" as a sufficient design remedy.
