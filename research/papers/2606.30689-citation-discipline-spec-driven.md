# Citation Discipline in Spec-Driven Development: A Cross-Model Empirical Study of Output Determinism and Automated Hallucination Detection in LLM-Generated Code

> Source: arXiv:2606.30689 (2026-06-28). Subham Panda. PDF in `research/arXiv/2606.30689-citation-discipline-spec-driven.pdf`.

## Thesis
Spec-Driven Development (SDD) frameworks differ in how they enforce traceability between requirements and generated code, and this choice has measurable, competing consequences. Through two pre-registered controlled studies across two frontier LLMs, the paper shows that mandatory per-line requirement citations (traceSDD) reduce output determinism yet are the only mechanism that enables automated hallucination detection. Citation annotations therefore trade lexical consistency for verifiability, and this trade-off replicates across model architectures.

## Key findings
- Mandatory inline citations significantly *reduce* determinism versus an identical uncited spec: Claude d = -0.76, p = 0.003; GLM d = -0.72, p < 0.001 (abstract; §5.2.1) — an LSS drop of ~0.21 (Claude) and ~0.13 (GLM) points.
- Only the cited condition enables automated hallucination detection: TDR = 86.4% (Claude), 88.0% (GLM), versus 0% for uncited traceSDD, Spec Kit, and OpenSpec, all at 0% false-positive rate (§6.1, §6.3, Table 3).
- All four conditions achieve 100% functional correctness (TPR), so citation discipline neither helps nor hurts test-pass rates (§6.4, H3).
- Structured REQ-format specs anchor determinism even without citations: the uncited condition beats Spec Kit (mean d ≈ 1.02) and OpenSpec (mean d ≈ 0.91) (§9, finding 4).
- traceSDD (cited) outperforms Spec Kit on determinism (Claude d = 0.47, p = 0.049; GLM d = 0.42, p = 0.003) but not OpenSpec (non-significant, d = 0.18 / 0.14) (Table 2).
- The citation determinism penalty is largest for easy (d = -1.85) and small (d = -0.95) tasks, and not significant for hard (d = -0.34, ns) or large (d = -0.21, ns) tasks (§5.5, GLM N=50).
- Detection has a gap: when the model omits citations entirely for hallucinated lines (rather than citing a fake REQ ID) the orphan-REQ check misses them — ~12% of Claude cases (§6.5, §8).

## Relevance to the book
This is exactly the peer-reviewed empirical counterweight Chapter 3 needs against Ahuja's opinion writing on the vibe-coding vs SDD debate. It quantifies what SDD buys and costs: verifiability and traceability at the price of determinism, with 100% test-pass across all methods showing that "passes the tests" is not evidence of spec-conformance. It also directly extends Chapter 1's non-determinism thesis — the study measures LLM output variance across independent sessions and shows specification structure can only partly anchor it. Because Claude Sonnet 4.6 is one of the two tested models, the numbers ground claims about real agentic coding, not toy demos.

## Stance
pro-SDD (with a measured caveat) / empirical-vibe — an empirical study broadly favouring spec-driven, citation-disciplined development for verifiability, while conceding a real determinism cost and endorsing lighter uncited REQ-format specs for rapid prototyping.
