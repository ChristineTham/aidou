# 10.1145/3442188.3445922 — On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?

> Source: <https://doi.org/10.1145/3442188.3445922> — Emily M. Bender, Timnit Gebru, Angelina McMillan-Major, Shmargaret Shmitchell (2021). Published in FAccT '21: Proceedings of the 2021 ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency, pp. 610–623. (Not on arXiv; ACM Digital Library only.)

## Thesis
The paper argues that scaling language models ever larger carries costs and harms that scale with them. Environmentally, it cites Strubell et al.: training one Transformer (big) model with neural architecture search emitted an estimated 284 metric tons of CO2 (the widely quoted 626,155 lbs), against ~5 t CO2 for an average human per year, and training a single BERT-base model consumed roughly the energy of a trans-American flight. On bias, it shows that scale is not neutrality: uncurated web-scraped training data over-represents already-privileged voices — GPT-2 was trained on Reddit outbound links, and Pew found 67% of US Reddit users are men and 64% are 18–29, while surveys of Wikipedians find only 8.8–15% are women. The lesson: a large LM is a "stochastic parrot" that stitches together linguistic form without meaning, encoding and amplifying the biases of its training data at a scale that resists documentation and audit.

## Relevance to the book
The landmark/foundational source for the fairness-and-bias strand of Ch5, and the piece that popularised the argument that fluency is not understanding and that bigger is not automatically better. Use it to open the discussion of how LLM bias originates in skewed training corpora and why data provenance matters. It also supplies the environmental-cost angle for the responsibility argument. It sets up the tension the other two sources answer: Stochastic Parrots warns that scale defeats documentation, while Model Cards and SMACTR are the concrete transparency/audit responses.
