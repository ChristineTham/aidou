# ericsson-1993 — The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance

> Source: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.100.3.363 — K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Th. Krampe, Clemens Tesch-Römer (1993). *Psychological Review*, 100(3), 363–406.

## Thesis

Studying violinists and pianists at the Music Academy in Berlin, the authors found that accumulated hours of "deliberate practice" — effortful, feedback-driven activity specifically designed to improve performance, not mere experience — predicted skill level: the best violinists had logged roughly 10,000 hours of solitary practice by age 20, markedly more than less accomplished peers. They argue that expert performance is built over a minimum of ~10 years of sustained deliberate practice rather than fixed innate talent, and that the defining ingredient is the quality and intent of the practice, not just time on task. (This is the foundational source behind the later-popularised "10,000-hour rule".)

## Relevance to the book

Anchor source for Ch6 cluster (b) on expertise and deliberate practice — the canonical account of how durable human skill is actually built. It grounds the chapter's "forward practice" thesis: if expertise comes from effortful, feedback-rich work, then offloading that effort to AI (per Kosmyna and Gerlich) removes the very mechanism that produces mastery. Provides the constructive counterweight — what humans must keep doing deliberately even as AI handles routine production.
