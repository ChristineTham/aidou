# gerlich-2025 — AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking

> Source: https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006 — Michael Gerlich (2025). *Societies*, 15(1), 6.

## Thesis

A mixed-methods study of 666 UK participants (survey + critical-thinking assessment + interviews) found a significant negative correlation between frequent AI-tool use and critical-thinking scores, and showed statistically that the relationship is mediated by cognitive offloading — delegating mental work to the tool. Younger participants (17–25) reported higher AI dependence and scored lower on critical thinking than older groups, while higher educational attainment buffered the effect. The lesson is that habitual reliance on AI for reasoning tasks correlates with weaker independent reasoning, but reflective habits and education partly protect against it.

## Relevance to the book

Behavioural counterpart to the Kosmyna EEG study for Ch6 cluster (a): where Kosmyna shows a neural signature, Gerlich shows the correlated behavioural outcome (lower critical-thinking scores) and names the mechanism (cognitive offloading). Supports the chapter's argument that offloading is the specific risk to guard against, and its education/reflection buffer gives Ch6 a concrete lever for the "forward practice" prescription. Balance note: correlational, self-report exposure measure, single-country sample — establishes association, not causation.
