# 2001.00973 — Closing the AI Accountability Gap: Defining an End-to-End Framework for Internal Algorithmic Auditing

> Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00973> — Inioluwa Deborah Raji, Andrew Smart, Rebecca N. White, Margaret Mitchell, Timnit Gebru, Ben Hutchinson, Jamila Smith-Loud, Daniel Theron, Parker Barnes (2020). Published at ACM FAT* '20, DOI 10.1145/3351095.3372873. PDF in `pdf/2001.00973.pdf`.

## Thesis
Most algorithmic audits are *external* and happen after deployment, when harm has already occurred and is hard to trace to its source. The paper introduces SMACTR, a five-stage *internal* audit framework — Scoping, Mapping, Artifact Collection, Testing, and Reflection — applied throughout the development lifecycle, where each stage produces documentation (e.g. model cards, datasheets, failure-mode analyses) that together form an overall audit report checked against the organisation's stated principles. The lesson: accountability is a process embedded before release, not a one-off post-hoc test; the audit is deliberately "boring, slow, meticulous and methodical," antithetical to rapid AI development.

## Relevance to the book
Anchors the algorithmic-auditing section of Ch5 and connects it to governance-as-process. It supplies the concrete lifecycle framework (SMACTR) to cite when arguing that fairness/bias controls must be operationalised as gated, documented stages inside the org, not left to outside researchers. It also ties the transparency artifacts (model cards from 1810.03993, datasheets) into a single accountability chain, making it the structural backbone for the responsibility argument.
