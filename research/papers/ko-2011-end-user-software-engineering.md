# The State of the Art in End-User Software Engineering

> Source: ACM Computing Surveys 43(3), Article 21 (April 2011). DOI 10.1145/1922649.1922658. Ko, Abraham, Beckwith, Blackwell, Burnett, Erwig, Scaffidi, Lawrance, Lieberman, Myers, Rosson, Rothermel, Shaw, Wiedenbeck. Open PDF in `pdf/ko-2011-euse.pdf`.

## Thesis

Canonical survey of *end-user software engineering*. Key framing: "Most programs today are written not by professional software developers, but by people with expertise in other domains working towards goals for which they need computational support" — teachers with grading spreadsheets, scientists with scripts. These end-user programmers face the same software-engineering challenges as professionals: requirements, design, reuse, integration, testing, debugging.

## Relevance to the book

The historical anchor for Ch3 §"Who Builds the Software": domain experts have always written much of the world's software (the democratization arc), and they still hit the same engineering problems — supporting both the "domain expert builds" thesis and the "discipline still matters" balance.
