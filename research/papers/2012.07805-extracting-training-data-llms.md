# 2012.07805 — Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models

> Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07805> — Nicholas Carlini, Florian Tramèr, Eric Wallace, Matthew Jagielski, Ariel Herbert-Voss, Katherine Lee, Adam Roberts, Tom Brown, Dawn Song, Úlfar Erlingsson, Alina Oprea, Colin Raffel (Dec 2020; USENIX Security 2021). PDF in `pdf/2012.07805.pdf`.

## Thesis
The paper introduces the *training-data extraction attack*: an adversary with only black-box query access to a language model can recover verbatim individual training examples. Applied to GPT-2, the authors generated 1,800 candidate samples and confirmed over 600 as verbatim training data, including personally identifiable information (names, phone numbers, email addresses), IRC logs, code, and 128-bit UUIDs — even when a sequence appeared in only a single training document. The central lesson: memorization is not an edge case, and larger models are more vulnerable than smaller ones, so publishing a model trained on private data can leak that data.

## Relevance to the book
This is the landmark source for Chapter 5's privacy/data-protection section — the foundational demonstration that models memorize and leak PII. It grounds the claim that "your training data is not private just because the model is a black box" and motivates the whole discussion of data governance (what you feed a model can be exfiltrated). It pairs with 2311.17035 (the production-scale successor) and is balanced by 2110.05679 (differential privacy as a mitigation).
