# ABA Formal Opinion 512 — Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools

Source: https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2024/07/aba-issues-first-ethics-guidance-ai-tools/
Body: American Bar Association, Standing Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility
Opinion: Formal Opinion 512, "Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools"
Date: 29 July 2024 (the ABA's first formal ethics opinion on lawyers' use of generative AI)
(Substance compiled from the ABA news release and secondary legal summaries; the opinion PDF is the primary.)

## Overview

Formal Opinion 512 states that to protect clients, lawyers and law firms using generative AI (GAI) must "fully consider their applicable ethical obligations," which include the duties to provide competent legal representation, to protect client information, to communicate with clients, and to charge reasonable fees consistent with time spent using GAI. The opinion maps GAI use onto the ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct: competence, confidentiality, communication, candour toward tribunals, supervisory responsibilities, and reasonable fees.

## Competence (Model Rule 1.1)

Lawyers must exercise the "legal knowledge, skill, thoroughness and preparation reasonably necessary" for competent representation, and must understand "the benefits and risks associated" with the technologies they use. Applied to GAI, lawyers should have a reasonable and current understanding of the specific capabilities and limitations of any GAI tool they use. The verification required is fact-specific and "will necessarily depend on the GAI tool and the specific task that it performs" — the duty that answers the fabricated-citation problem directly.

## Confidentiality (Model Rule 1.6)

Before entering information related to a client's representation into a GAI tool, lawyers must assess the potential that the information will be "disclosed to or accessed by" others inside or outside the firm, including via the outputs of a "self-learning" tool used by others. Informed client consent is required before entering client information into a self-learning GAI tool, and that consent must go beyond "boiler-plate provisions" in engagement letters.

## Fees (Model Rule 1.5)

Time billed must reflect actual time spent, including for GAI use. Overhead such as a subscription fee for a GAI tool should not be billed to clients. Lawyers cannot charge clients for the time spent learning to use GAI tools, though they may charge if a client requests a specific tool.

## Supervision (Model Rules 5.1 and 5.3)

Managerial and supervisory lawyers must adopt measures ensuring all lawyers and non-lawyer assistants comply with the rules when using GAI, including clear firm policies and training on the ethical, practical, and other risks of AI use.
