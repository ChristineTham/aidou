# The Age Of Artificial Intelligence — Quinnipiac University National Poll

> Source: Quinnipiac University Poll, release #3923, 16 April 2025.
> URL: https://poll.qu.edu/poll-release?releaseid=3923
> Web page saved as Markdown for the book's research pipeline. Figures transcribed from the release.

## Headline

The Age Of Artificial Intelligence: Americans Wary Of Impact On Daily Life, See Harm To
Education, Benefits To Medical Advances, Quinnipiac University National Poll Finds; Americans
Expect AI Will Cause Job Losses But Workers Don't Think Their Own Jobs Will Disappear

## Introduction

As artificial intelligence rapidly changes how Americans live and work, the public holds mixed
feelings about AI's societal impact — with conflicting views on jobs but unified concerns about
effects on younger generations. Businesses and government are viewed as insufficient in their
roles regarding the technology, according to a Quinnipiac University national poll released
16 April 2025, conducted in collaboration with the School of Computing & Engineering and the
School of Business.

## Day-to-day impact of AI (more good vs more harm)

- Overall: **44%** think AI will do more harm than good in their day-to-day life; **38%** think
  more good than harm; **18%** did not offer an opinion.

**By household income:**

| Household income | More good than harm | More harm than good |
|---|---|---|
| Over $200,000 / year | **60%** | 30% |
| Under $50,000 / year | **26%** | **59%** |

The pattern: higher-income households lean positive on AI's daily-life impact; lower-income
households lean strongly negative.

## Education

More than half (**54%**) think AI will do more harm than good in education; **32%** think more
good than harm; **15%** offered no opinion.

## Medical advances

**59%** think AI will do more good than harm regarding medical advances; **24%** think more harm
than good; **17%** offered no opinion.

## Jobs — the "workforce paradox"

- A majority of Americans (**56%**) think advancements in AI are likely to lead to a **decrease**
  in the number of job opportunities for people; **24%** think it will make no difference;
  **13%** expect an increase in job opportunities.
- Yet among Americans who are **employed**, only **21%** say they are very concerned (6%) or
  somewhat concerned (15%) that AI may make their own jobs obsolete, while a **vast majority
  (78%)** say they are not so concerned (22%) or not concerned at all (56%). This figure is
  essentially unchanged from the 24 May 2023 poll.

So a clear majority expects AI to cut jobs in general, but almost four in five employed adults do
not think it threatens their own job — an expectation gap between the aggregate forecast and
personal risk.

## Comfort with AI making consequential decisions

Share saying they would be comfortable with AI being used to:

| Use of AI | Comfortable (yes) | Not comfortable (no) |
|---|---|---|
| Screen health insurance claims | 23% | 71% |
| Screen loan applications | 27% | 67% |
| Screen job applications | 30% | 64% |
| Assist police in identifying suspects | 53% | 42% |

The public is most resistant when AI touches personal outcomes (health coverage, loans, jobs).

## Expert commentary (Quinnipiac faculty)

- **Chetan Jaiswal, Ph.D.** (Associate Professor of Computer Science, School of Computing &
  Engineering): "Americans draw lines based on trust and who stands to gain or lose."
- **Tamilla Triantoro, Ph.D.** (Associate Professor of Business Analytics): "The public draws the
  line at AI influencing personal outcomes — jobs, loans, and healthcare."

## Methodology

From 3–7 April 2025, Quinnipiac University surveyed **1,562 U.S. adults** nationwide with a
margin of error of **±2.5 percentage points**. The employed-adults subsample was **867** with a
margin of error of ±3.3 percentage points. The Quinnipiac University Poll has been directed by
Doug Schwartz, Ph.D., since 1994, and conducts independent, non-partisan national surveys.
