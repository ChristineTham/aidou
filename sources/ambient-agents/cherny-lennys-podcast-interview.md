## Verifiable extracts (short, plainly-correct phrases)

On working with agents running unattended:

> I personally haven't really felt it just cuz I have agents running all the time. Um, and I'm also
> just like not locked into a terminal anymore. Maybe a third of my code now is in the terminal but
> also a third is uh using the desktop app and then a third is the iOS app

On long-running agents:

> and they can also run for hours or even days at a time. I think there's some examples where they
> ran for many weeks. And so, I think over time this is going to become more and more normal, where
> the models are running for a very, very long period of time, and you don't have to sit there and
> baby sit them anymore.

(The preceding clause reads "always have a bunch of quads running" — an auto-caption mis-hearing of
"Claudes". Note also that "baby sit" is the captioner's word-split, not the speaker's spelling: speech
has no orthography, and Cherny's own written material uses "babysit". The book normalises it to
"babysit" when quoting this line — no change of meaning, and it avoids printing a machine artefact
as though it were his.)

A concrete ambient-agent example — chasing weekly status updates:

> we have a single spreadsheet for the whole team. There's like a row per engineer. Every week
> everyone fills out a status. And every Monday Cowork just goes through and it messages every
> engineer on Slack that hasn't filled out their status. And so, I don't have to do this anymore.

On running work in parallel:

> And then the third thing is just run a bunch of clouds in parallel. So, we can Cowork you can have
> as many tasks running as you want. So, it's like start one task … then something else, and then
> I'll kick these off.

("clouds" is again "Claudes".)

Other notable, verifiable points:

- On the pace of change: engineers should "transport yourself to the current moment and not get
  stuck back in in old model because it's not Sonnet 3.5 anymore."
- A prediction he made in May 2025 — that by the end of that year "you might not need an IDE to code
  anymore" — after which he recalls the room "audibly gasped".
- On his own experience: "I don't have to do the tedious work anymore of coding."
