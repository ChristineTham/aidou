# Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence (Executive Order 14365)

- Source URL: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/
- Author: The White House (President Donald J. Trump)
- Date: 11 December 2025
- Type: US presidential executive order (primary source). Not enacted legislation.

## Extracted facts

Executive Order 14365, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence,"
signed 11 December 2025, seeks to establish federal primacy over AI regulation and to preempt
state-level AI laws the administration views as obstructing a national policy. Key mechanisms:

- **AI Litigation Task Force.** The Attorney General must, within 30 days, establish a task
  force to challenge state AI laws in federal court on grounds such as unconstitutional burden
  on interstate commerce or federal preemption. (Task force operative from ~10 January 2026.)
- **Identify "onerous" state laws.** The Secretary of Commerce has 90 days to identify state
  AI laws that are burdensome — particularly those requiring AI models to alter their "truthful"
  outputs.
- **FTC angle.** State laws requiring alterations to truthful AI outputs framed as potentially
  preempted by the FTC Act's prohibition on deceptive practices.
- **Funding leverage.** Commerce to condition some federal broadband (BEAD non-deployment)
  funding on states not enforcing "onerous" AI laws.
- **Legislative proposal.** Officials directed to draft uniform federal AI legislation to
  preempt conflicting state laws, with carve-outs (child safety, data-centre infrastructure,
  state procurement/use of AI).

Follow-on: on 20 March 2026 the administration released a four-page "National Policy Framework
for Artificial Intelligence" urging Congress to enact a single federal standard preempting
state law. As of mid-2026, no comprehensive federal AI statute has been enacted; states
continued passing AI laws despite the order.

## Relation to the book

Directly updates §5.2.3 ("Around the World"), whose US passage says the United States "has no
federal AI statute; what it has is a patchwork of state laws." Still literally true, but now
incomplete: the federal government is moving by executive action to override the state patchwork.
Also connects to §5.2.4 ("Counting the Cost") — fragmentation of state rules is the argument the
preemption push leans on.
